Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The PRC Company Law imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a standard consulting WFOE and no blocked escrow account is required. Under Article 47, shareholders must contribute their subscribed registered capital in full within five years from the date of establishment unless laws, administrative regulations, or State Council rules impose a shorter period. Registered capital may therefore be recorded as subscribed, but it must be genuinely paid according to the contribution schedule and used only within the approved business scope. For this AEO supply-chain audit advisory WFOE, the registered capital should be set at a realistic level proportionate to the consulting engagement pipeline and contingent professional liability exposure.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for a Civil Defense fire permit or industrial occupancy clearance because the WFOE is a pure ISIC 7020 consulting service office with no manufacturing, chemical, assembly, warehousing or hazardous storage. The landlord must nevertheless provide an office building with legal fire safety acceptance or a valid fire inspection certificate. The company must comply with the PRC Fire Protection Law and the Work Safety Law by maintaining unobstructed escape routes, portable fire extinguishers, emergency lighting and workplace safety measures appropriate to a low-risk office environment.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic portal: STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) and the China International Trade Single Window for export/customs reporting, with General Administration of Customs (GACC) as the co-competent authority for customs duties and export tax refund verification.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) with the State Administration for Market Regulation; the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued on the business license functions as the WFOE's statutory taxpayer identification number for VAT, enterprise income tax, stamp duty and withholding tax. Step 2: Complete post-incorporation tax registration with the in-charge STA office through the Electronic Tax Bureau within the statutory 30-day period; verify the identities of the legal representative, financial officer and tax agent; declare tax type(s) and filing cadence; file the bank account in the taxpayer bank deposit account record; and execute a tax-payment direct debit agreement if applicable. Step 3: For foreign non-resident shareholders or counterparties without a permanent establishment in China, the WFOE as payer must complete contract filing in the STA non-resident enterprise tax administration system; after verification the non-resident entity is assigned a non-resident taxpayer identification number and the WFOE issues withholding tax certificates for China-source dividends, interest, royalties or fees. Step 4: If the non-resident entity maintains a branch or fixed place of business in mainland China, that establishment must separately register in its local STA jurisdiction and obtain its own mainland China tax registration using the foreign enterprise registration documents; no generic check-the-box entity classification is available.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC tax law does not provide a pass-through or check-the-box election for WFOEs. A WFOE is always a resident enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide income. It may elect small-scale VAT treatment until the statutory turnover threshold is crossed, and it may elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer before the threshold; no hybrid mismatch check-the-box election is available in the mainland China tax system.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration for a WFOE is triggered by annual taxable sales exceeding CNY 5,000,000 (RMB 5 million); below that threshold the WFOE is classified as a small-scale taxpayer unless voluntary general VAT registration is made.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of physical goods qualify for 0% VAT only if a general VAT taxpayer exports goods under a valid customs export declaration, the goods physically leave mainland China, the exporter retains legal documentation and receives payment in foreign exchange. Qualifying cross-border services are zero-rated only if they appear on the STA zero-rated export service schedule (e.g., certain international transportation, design, R&D and software services) and are supplied to non-resident overseas customers with consumption wholly outside China; other exported consulting and audit services not on the zero-rated schedule may qualify only for the cross-border service VAT exemption rather than 0% VAT.

  • Customs export declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form with GACC customs number) containing export commodity code, quantity, value and destination
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent international transport document (air waybill, road or rail consignment note) proving physical export from China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) and/or bank export foreign-exchange receipt and settlement verification slip evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice signed by the WFOE and customs valuation/packing list declarations
  • Export sales contract or order confirmation identifying buyer, Incoterms, currency and payment terms
  • Certificate of origin or export licence where the product category or preferential trade claim requires it

VAT invoicing is controlled by the STA Golden Tax Phase IV infrastructure. The WFOE must issue invoices through the STA-authorized e-invoice system using the digital tax-control U-Key or Full-Process Digitalized Electronic Invoice (DEI) platform for real-time issuance. Invoice data flow to the STA over secure API channels with automatic invoice code assignment, anti-fraud risk marking and fiscal clearance matching; special VAT invoices cannot be manually hand-written or retroactively amended without STA re-issuance procedures.

Export zero-rating is audited by STA and GACC using customs declarations, bill-of-lading data and bank foreign-exchange records. Missing, inconsistent or fraudulent documentation, failure to repatriate foreign exchange within the applicable deadline, circular financing, or material mismatches between the FIRC, customs declaration and B/L will reclassify the transaction as a domestic taxable sale; the VAT at the standard rate is assessed, input credits are reversed, refunded amounts are clawed back with late-payment surcharge, administrative penalties of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid are imposed under Article 63 of the Law on Tax Administration and Collection, and criminal liability for fraudulent export refunds may attach. In addition, the WFOE's customs AEO certification can be suspended or revoked and its credit rating downgraded, resulting in mandatory inspection and loss of expedited clearance privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a substantive foreign exchange control system administered by SAFE under PBOC authority. Current account transactions are broadly convertible on a real-need and document-verified basis; the WFOE may settle legitimate trade receivables, service fees, royalties, and dividends through a designated bank using contracts, invoices, customs declarations, tax certificates, and audited financial statements without any separate SAFE approval. Capital account transactions remain tightly managed and event-driven; an inbound equity injection from the Hong Kong parent requires an FDI foreign exchange registration before remittance, and any shareholder loan requires SAFE debt registration or a cross-border financing macro-prudential filing. RMB converted from foreign currency capital must be applied within the entity's registered business scope and cannot be used for prohibited investment activities, entrusted loans, securities investments, or repayment of non-operational borrowings. Dividend remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is treated as current account profit distribution, but only after the bank verifies the audited profit, corporate income tax settlement status, board resolution on distribution, and the WFOE's foreign exchange compliance standing; the HK-China double tax treaty may reduce the dividend withholding rate to 5 percent only if the Hong Kong shareholder passes the beneficial ownership and substance test. The overall regime is therefore convertible for genuine operating and dividend flows, but liquidity cannot be treated as freely mobile capital: every inbound or outbound movement must carry compliant supporting evidence and be routed through a supervised onshore bank account. Profit remittance hurdles include the requirement to prove that distributable profit was fully taxed, that the foreign exchange account history is free of B/C-class irregularities, and that the distribution resolution does not exceed cumulative retained earnings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 2012 goods trade foreign exchange reform, there is no fixed statutory surrender ratio for export proceeds in China. A WFOE may retain 100 percent of collected export foreign currency in its foreign exchange settlement account or voluntarily convert the funds into RMB at its designated bank. Mandatory repatriation remains in force: the export receivables must be collected into a domestic foreign exchange account, and the standard monitoring window for post-customs collection is 90 days from the date of export declaration. Where the commercial contract allows a longer payment term, the extension must be proactively reported in the SAFE monitoring system before the 90-day baseline expires. For service export revenue, including AEO supply-chain audit and consulting fees, the same current account verification principles apply: funds must be repatriated through the banking system and the underlying service contract, workpapers, invoice, and proof of delivery must be retained for SAFE post-event examination. Upon receipt, the WFOE may elect to hold or settle the foreign currency, but the bank will continue to monitor the trade verification period; any unexplained delay in collection or settlement with no supporting contract will expose the entity to a lower SAFE risk classification, mandatory transaction-by-transaction review, and restrictions on the use of electronic settlement.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the supreme monetary authority, with delegated foreign exchange supervisory authority exercised by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low and legally not viable as the primary onshore banking layer. Foreign e-money institutions and digital EMI platforms, including Wise, hold no PRC commercial banking licence and no PBOC domestic payment institution licence; they are therefore legally barred from opening local RMB current accounts, taking deposits, or executing domestic RMB payments on behalf of a mainland China WFOE. Wise can only be considered as an ancillary offshore payment tool after the WFOE has already moved funds through an onshore Tier-1 bank to the Hong Kong parent's licensed Hong Kong account, and even then it should not carry statutory tax, SAFE, or AEO audit obligations. Treat the WFOE's treasury backbone as the onshore bank account; any foreign EMI remittance must be supplementary and must be mirrored by a compliant onshore SWIFT or CIPS wire to maintain the full evidentiary chain required by SAFE and the PRC tax authorities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE must first complete the SAFE foreign direct investment registration through its designated onshore bank, recording the HK parent equity contribution under the correct FDI code. The Hong Kong parent then wires funds through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the WFOE's Capital Foreign Exchange Settlement Account at the Tier-1 bank, embedding the SAFE FDI registration number and the stated purpose code of equity injection in the remittance narrative. The receiving bank verifies the remitter exactly matches the registered shareholder, checks the underlying board resolution and articles of association, matches the amount to the FDI filing, and conducts source-of-funds due diligence before crediting the account. If the capital is to be converted into RMB for operational use, the WFOE must complete the capital-account foreign exchange settlement declaration, and the resulting RMB can only be used within the registered business scope, not for securities investment, entrusted loans, related-party lending, or repayment of unauthorised external debt. Capital verification is satisfied by the bank issuing an inward capital contribution confirmation and recording the receipt in the SAFE system; no separate government registry stamp is required for a standard limited liability WFOE, but the bank's confirmation is the key treasury audit evidence.For direct payment of operating expenses, supplier invoices, and overseas trade obligations, the WFOE routes funds through an onshore bank using SWIFT MT103 with the applicable SAFE trade purpose code, attaching the contract, invoice, customs declaration, and logistics documents. For larger import or capital-equipment purchases, the onshore bank issues commercial letters of credit via SWIFT MT700 and, when needed, MT707 amendments, with the WFOE as applicant and the foreign supplier as beneficiary. For documentary collection, the bank handles sight or term drafts under ICC URC 522 and the escrowed title documents such as original bills of lading, insurance certificates, and inspection reports. For cross-border RMB transactions, the WFOE may instruct disbursement through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) or SWIFT in RMB, while domestic RMB obligations on taxes, payroll, and local operating costs must be settled through PBOC's CNAPS high-value payment system at the onshore bank. Each outbound payment must retain a coherent audit trail of purpose code, signed mandate, authorised signatory verification, underlying transaction documentation, and, for dividend outbound flows, the profit distribution resolution and tax clearance certificate. The AEO certification may speed up documentary handling but does not remove SAFE's requirement to keep truthful supporting evidence for every cross-border remittance.Onshore deliverable CNY/USD FX forward contracts for genuine current account settlement, export proceeds, import payments, and known dividend flows under the SAFE real-need principleBank-executed FX swaps and swap rollovers used solely to match the timing of foreign currency receipts and liabilities arising from the WFOE's verified cross-border trade or financing exposureCross-currency interest rate swaps where the WFOE has a qualified cross-border shareholder loan or external borrowing and the swap is booked against the documented interest and principal exposureOffshore CNY non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) transacted by the Hong Kong parent for hedging its own PRC exposure, while the onshore WFOE itself may not access NDFs under current SAFE rulesOption-based currency protection structured as plain vanilla FX options or collar strategies offered by licensed onshore banks, provided the notional amount is measured against current, verified future receipts or payments

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low and legally not viable as the primary onshore banking layer. Foreign e-money institutions and digital EMI platforms, including Wise, hold no PRC commercial banking licence and no PBOC domestic payment institution licence; they are therefore legally barred from opening local RMB current accounts, taking deposits, or executing domestic RMB payments on behalf of a mainland China WFOE. Wise can only be considered as an ancillary offshore payment tool after the WFOE has already moved funds through an onshore Tier-1 bank to the Hong Kong parent's licensed Hong Kong account, and even then it should not carry statutory tax, SAFE, or AEO audit obligations. Treat the WFOE's treasury backbone as the onshore bank account; any foreign EMI remittance must be supplementary and must be mirrored by a compliant onshore SWIFT or CIPS wire to maintain the full evidentiary chain required by SAFE and the PRC tax authorities.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Every Hong Kong incorporated company must have its annual financial statements audited unless it qualifies as a 'small company' or 'small group' under Section 359 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). A private company satisfies the small company criteria if it meets at least two of the three following conditions for a financial year: (a) total revenue not exceeding HK$10 million (US$1.28 million); (b) total assets not exceeding HK$10 million (US$1.28 million); and (c) average number of employees not exceeding 50. If exempt, the company must still pass a solvency test and obtain unanimous shareholder approval. Additionally, under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, any company with gross income exceeding HK$2 million (US$256,410) must submit audited financial statements with its Profits Tax Return. Companies exceeding these thresholds, or those that do not opt for the solvency exemption, are mandatorily required to appoint a certified public accountant to conduct an annual statutory audit.
