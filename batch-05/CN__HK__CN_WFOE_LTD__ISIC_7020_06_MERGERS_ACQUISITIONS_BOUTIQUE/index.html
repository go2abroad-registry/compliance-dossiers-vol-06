Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement. The subscriber must allot at least one share; the board may issue shares credited as paid or unpaid. For banking and SFC Type 6 licensing purposes, the board resolution requires the initial ordinary shares to be fully paid in cash into the licensed bank account, but the Companies Registry and Business Registration Office do not impose any escrow, capital verification or deposit-blocking condition. If a mainland China WFOE subsidiary is later formed under PRC law, no minimum registered capital is imposed for consulting activities, subject to the capital contribution schedule in its articles of association.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for Civil Defense fire permits because the M&A advisory boutique operates no manufacturing, chemical, assembly, or warehousing premises. The office premises, however, must comply with the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95), obtain a Fire Service Installations certificate from a registered fire service installation contractor, and comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509) for workplace safety, fire escape routes and emergency evacuation planning.

Paid-in capital rule: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement. Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and its Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax) online service platform; local tax service bureaus act as the in-charge authorities for WFOE registration and compliance. The Hong Kong SAR Inland Revenue Department is not the registration authority for the mainland China WFOE.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in mainland China, tax registration is completed automatically through the one-stop incorporation procedure with the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR). The business license bears the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory tax identification number (TIN) and VAT taxpayer identification number for the enterprise. The AMR data relay makes a separate tax registration application unnecessary. The WFOE must then activate its Electronic Tax Bureau account, complete legal-representative and financial officer identity verification, and file bank account details. A foreign non-resident enterprise (including a Hong Kong holding company) that has China-source income but no establishment in China must complete a separate non-resident enterprise tax registration with the in-charge tax authority to obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification number for withholding obligations; a shareholder that merely holds equity interest in the WFOE and receives dividends from the WFOE is generally subject to withholding through the WFOE without a standalone registration file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC Corporate Income Tax Law does not permit pass-through or check-the-box elections for a WFOE. The WFOE is a resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income at the entity level; anti-hybrid and general anti-avoidance provisions, including CFC, thin-capitalisation and substance-over-form rules, apply. The Hong Kong parent's possible treaty protection under the Mainland and Hong Kong tax arrangement is a benefits claim, not a classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is automatic upon WFOE incorporation; the statutory general taxpayer threshold is RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable turnover. Above the threshold, general VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory; below it, small-scale taxpayer treatment (currently 3% or any legislated reduced levy) is available, with an option to elect general taxpayer status for input VAT credits. The M&A advisory service is subject to 6% VAT when supplied domestically.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for physical goods exported from mainland China upon actual customs export, and for cross-border services (including M&A advisory/consulting) supplied to an overseas recipient where the service is wholly consumed outside mainland China and the consideration is received from abroad in foreign currency. The statutory framework includes Cai Shui [2016] No. 36 and the State Taxation Administration announcements on cross-border taxable services zero-rating and exemption. Pure domestic onshore services and services consumed inside mainland China do not qualify.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods declaration) issued by China Customs for physical exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export shipment
  • Inward Remittance Receipt / Bank Credit Advice evidencing receipt of overseas payment in foreign currency
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Declaration Form/Bank Export Collection Verification Advice filed under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) system
  • Cross-border service contract between the WFOE and the overseas client specifying the service, counterparty, and place of performance
  • Commercial invoice issued by the WFOE and the VAT zero-rating/export filing record submitted through the Electronic Tax Bureau

China operates a mandatory electronic invoice regime through the national Electronic Invoice Service Platform and the Digital Electronic Invoice system, integrated with the Golden Tax Phase IV data platform. The WFOE must issue VAT invoices through the Electronic Tax Bureau or an authorised API/ERP integration; each invoice carries a nationally assigned invoice code, tax-control digital signature/stamp, and real-time data submission. Invoice data is cross-checked against VAT returns, customs declarations, banking records, and foreign-exchange collection data. Invoices lacking the digital stamp or not filed through the approved platform are non-compliant for input VAT credit and zero-rating substantiation.

If the export zero-rating claim is not substantiated by genuine export or cross-border supply, or is supported by false documentation, the STA will reverse the zero-rating, claw back the VAT refund, impose late-payment surcharge, and assess a penalty of at least 50% and up to five times the tax involved under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent claims for export VAT refunds also constitute the criminal offence of fraudulent export tax refund, carrying fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment. Unapproved retrospective audit and unlimited lookback apply where fraud or tax evasion is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates under a managed floating exchange-rate regime with current-account RMB convertibility accepted under IMF Article VIII, whereas capital-account transactions remain tightly controlled by SAFE registration and approval requirements. For a Hong Kong-owned PRC WFOE, inbound registered capital is classified as capital-account FDI and must be filed with SAFE before remittance. Once the remittance is received, the settlement bank verifies the Foreign Investment Information Report and may convert foreign exchange into RMB for approved operating purposes. Current-account outflows, including cross-border M&A-advisory fees, office rentals, data-subscription costs and external consultant payments, are permissible where supported by genuine contracts, invoices, tax certificates and the bank's customer classification. Profit repatriation to the Hong Kong parent requires annual audited financial statements, completed corporate income tax settlement, a board dividend resolution, and a SAFE payment-cum-export/income declaration. The principal hurdles are documentary substantiation, enhanced compliance review due to the medium-risk advisory classification, and anti-circumvention checks ensuring the underlying transactions are not disguised financing. Purely financial flows without economic substance are not permitted. Therefore the WFOE should maintain full transparency from engagement contracts and invoicing through tax settlement and SAFE filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's trade-facilitation framework there is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; eligible current-account foreign-exchange receipts may be retained 100% in an FX account or sold to a licensed bank. Export/service-receipt proceeds must be repatriated to a licensed PRC bank within 90 days of the related customs declaration or service-performance date, and delayed receipts must be explained to SAFE through the cross-border receipt and payment verification platform.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBC) and its delegated State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable for the mainland WFOE. Foreign fintech EMIs and offshore digital banking platforms do not hold PRC banking licences and are not authorised to operate RMB accounts, process SAFE-controlled FX settlements, or serve as designated foreign-exchange banks for a PRC WFOE. The WFOE must route domestic payroll, VAT, corporate tax, fund custody, and capital-account flows through a licensed PRC commercial bank. An overseas EMI may support only the Hong Kong parent's treasury side, and cannot substitute for the PRC treasury-clearing and SAFE reporting obligations. Feasibility rating: Low / Legally Unavailable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be remitted directly from the Hong Kong parent to the WFOE's capital-account at a Tier-1 PRC bank via SWIFT MT103. The remittance shall carry the SAFE-approved FDI/purpose code for registered capital injection, supported by the Foreign Investment Information Report and the SAFE FDI registration form. The receiving bank clears the funds through the SAFE capital-account information system, confirms the remitter matches the registered shareholder, and may settle foreign currency into RMB only for permitted operating expenditures. A licensed CPA must then issue a capital-verification report, and all downstream spending must be declared to SAFE, with invoices and contracts retained. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory 10% on dividend distributions from the WFOE to any non-resident corporate shareholder. Under the China–Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement (DTA), the rate is reduced to 5% provided the Hong Kong resident shareholder directly holds at least 25% of the capital of the WFOE and is the beneficial owner of the dividends. The beneficial owner test requires economic substance in Hong Kong and not simply a conduit holding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The WFOE must prepare a Local File if its related-party transactions for the year exceed: (i) RMB 200 million for transfers of tangible property; (ii) RMB 100 million for financial assets; (iii) RMB 100 million for transfers of intangible property; or (iv) RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions (including service fees and management fees). The Master File must be prepared if the total amount of related-party transactions exceeds RMB 1 billion. For the M&A advisory boutique, most intercompany service fees fall under the RMB 40 million threshold. If the total fee payments to Hong Kong exceed RMB 40 million, prepare the Local File and include a benchmarking analysis for the advisory services.

Economic substance scope: China has not legislated a separate Economic Substance Regulation like the BVI or Cayman, but the GAAR (Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law) and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures (SAT Notice 2017-6) require economic substance for intercompany arrangements. The ‘Beneficial Owner’ concept within the China–Hong Kong DTA imposes a substance-over-form rule: a Hong Kong shareholder or service provider has no treaty benefits unless it performs real business activities, has full authority to dispose of the income, and avoids asset-liability mismatches. For the WFOE, core income-generating activities (CIGA) must take place in China, including client advisory, deal structured, negotiation, and execution, because the WFOE is the resident entity with its effective management and key personnel in China. A Hong Kong entity must not be used merely as an invoice-passing vehicle; otherwise the tax authority will recharacterize the transaction and impose 10% withholding on the entire recharacterized amount.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Disguised Dividend

Statutory sanction: Under Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Notice 2016-42, the tax authority may recharacterize the fee as a disguised dividend and recalculate the corporate tax at 25%. Additionally, the recharacterized dividend is subject to 10% (or 5% if unfairly claimed treaty) withholding tax. A penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax is imposed under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law. A transfer pricing penalty of 15% of the adjustment amount may also apply (Article 121 of the Implementation Regulation).

Director personal liability: The legal representative and the financial director who approved the sham service contract may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax under Article 91 of the Tax Collection Administration Law if they intentionally caused the tax underpayment. They are subject to civil asset restitution (seizure of personal bank accounts and real estate). If the underpaid tax exceeds RMB 500,000 and constitutes tax evasion, the director can face criminal liability under Article 203 of the Criminal Law (up to 7 years’ imprisonment) and an unlimited fine. The director also faces a travel ban and placement on the personal ‘seriously dishonest taxpayer’ blacklist, preventing exit from China.
Treaty Abuse and Shell Company Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Under Article 20 of the Tax Collection Administration Law and SAT Notice 2009-601 (and the revised beneficial ownership test of 2018-9), the tax authority will deny the treaty benefit and demand the 10% statutory dividend WHT on the full amount. An additional penalty of 50% of the under-withheld tax (i.e., potentially RMB 400,000) may be imposed. The interest for late payment is 0.05% per day from the original due date. If fraud is proven, the criminal threshold is met.

Director personal liability: The director who signed the misleading treaty declaration may be pursued for assisting in tax fraud. Personal liability includes personal asset seizure for the unpaid tax and interest. The director will be blacklisted from leaving mainland China (travel ban) and may be subject to an immigration hold. For deliberate abuse, the case can be transferred to the anti-smuggling and tax police; the director may be sentenced to up to 15 years for tax fraud under Article 204 (if the amount is especially huge) or under the Money Laundering Law if the dividend is routed offshore.
Failure to Withhold WHT on Outbound Payments

Statutory sanction: As the withholding agent, the WFOE is liable for the tax that should have been withheld. Article 69 of the Tax Collection Administration Law imposes a penalty equal to 50% to 3 times the amount of tax not withheld. The tax authority will order the WFOE to pay the unpaid tax, plus a 0.05% daily surcharge from the due date. Additionally, the same transaction may be treated as services rendered in China if the consultant performs part of the M&A advisory activity within the PRC (presence test), in which case the WFOE must register and pay VAT at 6% or 3%, plus WHT on the deemed profit.

Director personal liability: The financial controller and the legal representative are jointly liable for the withheld tax under Article 139 of the Tax Collection Administration Law Enforcement Rules. The director’s personal property may be attached to satisfy the shortfall. For a willful failure exceeding RMB 300,000, criminal liability under Article 82 of the Criminal Law (tax evasion) applies, with imprisonment of up to 7 years and confiscation of personal property. The director is also subject to an exit ban and may be detained at customs when leaving China.
