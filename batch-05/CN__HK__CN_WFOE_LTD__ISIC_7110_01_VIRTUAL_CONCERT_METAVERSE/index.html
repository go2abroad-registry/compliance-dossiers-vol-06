Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC tax system provides no check-the-box, pass-through, or disregarded-entity election for a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise. The WFOE is mandatorily a resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law and taxed on worldwide income at the statutory 25% rate. Anti-hybrid mismatch and controlled foreign corporation rules apply through the general anti-avoidance provisions and applicable double tax treaties.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All VAT taxable entities must register when they commence taxable activities in China. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer classification is triggered when annual VAT taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. A WFOE supplying virtual performance hosting and digital event ticketing services below this threshold may remain a small-scale taxpayer (simplified levy rate), but cross-border service operations with input VAT recovery will normally require general taxpayer status at the standard services rate of 6%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% VAT applies to exports that are customs-cleared and removed from the customs territory of China, supported by the export declaration and proof of foreign exchange receipt. For cross-border services, including virtual performance hosting, digital ticket tokenization, and metaverse event delivery, zero-rating applies under Circular Caishui [2016] No. 36 and subsequent rules only where the services are supplied to a non-resident enterprise or individual, are wholly consumed outside China, and consideration is received in foreign exchange. The taxpayer must maintain a written contract, delivery logs, IP or platform usage records, technical description, and a foreign-exchange receipt record demonstrating the offshore nature of the supply. If the service is delivered to or consumed by persons in China, 6% VAT applies and zero-rating is denied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry), Bill of Lading, Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)

China mandates electronic invoicing and real-time fiscal clearance under the Golden Tax System Phase IV (Jin Shui Phase IV). VAT invoices must be generated through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau or an authorized fully digitalized electronic invoice (FDEI) platform. Invoice data are transmitted to the STA through APIs, and each invoice carries a unique invoice code, anti-fraud QR code, and digital stamp. Invoice quotas are administratively configured, and cancellations or red-ink invoices require digital authorization.

Export VAT zero-rating and refund claims are subject to joint audits by the State Taxation Administration and General Administration of Customs. If the required customs documents, foreign-exchange repatriation records, or offshore consumption evidence are missing or false, the claimed 0% rate is clawed back and VAT is assessed at the applicable domestic rate (6% for services) plus late-payment surcharge. Fraudulent export refund claims may result in administrative fines ranging from 50% to five times the refunded tax, suspension or revocation of taxpayer qualification, inclusion in the tax non-compliant list, and criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. A 180-day deadline is enforced for receipt and repatriation of export proceeds; failure to repatriate within the deadline triggers mandatory reassessment and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The WFOE operates under the Renminbi non-deliverable capital controls regime. Current-account items are broadly convertible, allowing trade in services and goods, as long as each transaction has a genuine underlying commercial contract and the required SAFE declaration codes. Capital-account items remain materially restricted; foreign direct investment into the WFOE must be registered with SAFE before any injection, and capital contributions must be paid into a dedicated foreign-investment capital account at a designated Chinese bank. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is permitted as an outward-repatriation dividend, but requires annual audited financial statements, a board resolution, a certificate of survival, and full settlement of corporate income tax withholding. There is no explicit convertibility ceiling for lawful dividends, but the bank must be able to evidence remittance intent, which makes capital controls transparent rather than prohibitive. Retail payment flow from virtual concert tickets is treated as current-account cross-border e-commerce revenue and must be routed through an approved bank gateway or licensed payment institution. Cryptocurrency, including USDC, cannot be used as a direct funding rail due to the PBoC digital-asset prohibition; all crypto must be liquidated outside China before entering the treasury system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China does not impose a statutory mandatory conversion ratio on export foreign exchange receipts. The WFOE may retain up to 100 percent of foreign-currency trade proceeds in a designated export-receipts account at a qualified domestic bank, provided all proceeds are repatriated into China and matched to the corresponding customs declaration through the SAFE cross-border electronic verification system. The repatriation deadline is generally 30 days from the date the foreign exchange is credited to the banking system, with the customs/shipping window closing within 180 days of shipment under standard trade-verification practice. Banks may apply their own internal settlement policies requiring a portion to be converted into RMB, but that is a counterparty credit decision rather than a fixed SAFE surrender ratio.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBoC)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must first obtain a SAFE FDI registration code for the WFOE. The capital contribution is then wired via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE dedicated foreign exchange capital account at a Tier-1 Chinese bank, using the applicable direct-investment purpose code. The receiving bank verifies the presence of the SAFE registration, confirms the capital contribution amount, issues a foreign currency capital receipt, and records the injection in the SAFE digital control table. After conversion into RMB, the bank verifies the WFOE business license and industry restrictions, performs the capital payment confirmation process, and sweeps funds into the RMB basic operational account. A qualified accountant or the bank issues the capital verification statement required by Chinese company-law practice. They may function as Medium-viability front-end collection aggregators only when traffic is routed through a licensed Chinese bank or PBoC-licensed payment institution using SAFE cross-border e-commerce approvals. Any collection in USDC must be off-ramped to fiat outside mainland China through a licensed Hong Kong VASP before the resulting fiat wire can be cleared into the WFOE banking stack; direct USDC settlement into Chinese bank rails is prohibited.
