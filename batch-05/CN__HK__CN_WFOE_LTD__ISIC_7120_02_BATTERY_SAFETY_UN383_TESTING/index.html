Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online service platform and the STA's provincial and municipal tax service bureaus.

E-government filing portal

For the China WFOE, the statutory taxpayer identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license. The non-resident shareholder first obtains notarized and apostilled or legalized incorporation documents, then registers the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) through the integrated business registration system; tax registration is completed concurrently upon issuance of the business license, and the WFOE must verify its tax registration and banking information in the e-Tax portal, and register as either a small-scale or general VAT taxpayer. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment may separately obtain a tax identification number for withholding and cross-border payment compliance by filing its overseas registration certificate, a Chinese tax representative appointment letter, and the underlying cross-border contract with the competent STA office.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from China and declared to customs may be zero-rated if the export proceeds are collected and the documentary chain is complete. Cross-border services, including qualifying UN38.3 battery testing and CE-RED electromagnetic testing outputs, may be zero-rated or exempt only where the service is supplied to an overseas beneficial recipient, is consumed entirely outside China, and falls within the STA cross-border taxable act zero-rating and exemption catalogue; mere issuance of an invoice to an overseas client is insufficient. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the testing service remains taxable at the 6% modern-services VAT rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration) for any physical goods, battery samples, or test articles exported from China
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing export movement
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and the underlying cross-border contract or purchase order
  • Foreign exchange receipt voucher (bank foreign exchange receipt certificate or FIRC) proving the export proceeds were repatriated to China
  • For service-only exports: written service agreement, final test report or certificate, proof of the overseas recipient's tax registration, and evidence that the service was wholly consumed outside China

Full Digital Electronic Invoices are issued through the STA national electronic invoice service platform and transmitted in real time to the Golden Tax Phase IV system for fiscal clearance. General VAT taxpayers must use approved e-invoicing systems, with certified service-provider API integration for bulk issuance; electronic signatures, QR codes, and encrypted digital stamping are mandatory anti-fraud controls. Special VAT invoices are subject to nationwide verification and automatic matching with VAT filings, customs export declarations, and foreign exchange receipts.

The STA cross-references the export customs declaration, e-invoice data, logistics documents, and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) receipt records in the Golden Tax Phase IV system. If zero-rating is not substantiated within the prescribed period, the WFOE must reverse the zero-rated output VAT and pay VAT at the applicable rate plus daily late-payment surcharge under the Tax Collection and Administration Law. Fraudulent export refund claims are clawed back with a fine of 100% to 500% of the amount fraudulently obtained, and criminal liability may be pursued in serious cases under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime in which the Renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible for current account items, such as trade in goods, services, profit remittance, and royalties, provided that the underlying transactions are genuine and supported by comprehensive documentary evidence. Capital account items, including inbound foreign direct investment, outbound equity investment, cross-border loans, and repatriation of capital, remain subject to strict SAFE registration, use-of-funds verification, and purpose-specific coding. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in China receives its equity funding through a dedicated FDI capital account, and each inward remittance from the Hong Kong parent must carry the correct SAFE payment purpose code and be recorded in SAFE's capital project management system. Once the FDI capital is settled to RMB, it may only be deployed within the entity's permitted business scope and cannot be arbitrarily redirected to non-operating investments. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is allowed as a current account payment, but the bank will require audited financial statements, a board-approved profit-distribution resolution, evidence that corporate income tax has been fully declared and paid, and a certified certificate for the distribution. There is no blanket approval from SAFE for ordinary dividend remittances, yet the bank may reject the payment if the WFOE has negative retained earnings, unresolved tax liabilities, or discrepancies between the declared revenue and the bank's transaction monitoring profile. The overarching practical hurdles are administrative burdens rather than outright prohibition: enterprises must maintain meticulous contemporaneous records of every cross-border payment, align invoices with customs and logistics documents, and keep the FX purpose code fully consistent with the underlying commercial contract.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender requirement or statutory conversion ratio is currently imposed on export proceeds under the post-2012 SAFE reform. Chinese resident enterprises are permitted to retain export foreign currency receipts in an onshore foreign exchange settlement account, but all such proceeds must be repatriated to China and recognised through the SAFE trade monitoring system. Receipts must be credited to the bank settlement account within the payment terms stated in the underlying commercial contract and customs declaration; banks then reconcile the amounts against export records, invoices, and bills of lading/airway bills. If the WFOE elects to convert export proceeds to RMB, conversion is freely available at market rates for legitimate current account transactions. If the WFOE retains the foreign currency, it must comply with the bank's current account foreign exchange account quota management and must be prepared to justify any unusual balance accumulation. There is thus no fixed statutory timeline such as a 30-day or 90-day surrender window, but a clear and auditable audit trail from invoice to receipt to settlement is mandatory.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Very low / not viable as a standalone treasury solution. Digital fintech EMIs such as Wise are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open RMB or foreign exchange settlement accounts for mainland-incorporated WFOEs. They do not operate within China's domestic payment and clearing infrastructure (CNAPS/CIPS), cannot accept FDI equity injections, and cannot perform the statutory SAFE capital registration and verification functions required for an operating WFOE. Some licensed Chinese fintech cross-border platforms may act as customer-facing channels in partnership with domestic commercial banks, but the account and clearing rail must ultimately reside at a licensed Chinese bank. For a battery-safety UN38.3 testing laboratory, where compliance verifiability and traceability are critical, relying on an EMI would create unacceptable regulatory and operational risk. Therefore, at least one Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank account must be the primary treasury backbone, and any EMI can only be used as an ancillary invoicing or multi-currency distribution tool outside mainland China.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required when annual related-party tangible asset transfers exceed CNY 200M, intangible asset or financial asset transfers exceed CNY 100M, or other related-party transactions exceed CNY 40M; Master File required when annual aggregate related-party transactions exceed CNY 1B.

Economic substance scope: For China WFOE income, the State Taxation Administration applies substance-over-form and GAAR under EIT Law Art. 47; the Hong Kong shareholder must satisfy beneficial ownership and CIGA in Hong Kong to obtain treaty benefits. If the HK entity is a letterbox holding company without board meetings, employees, offices, or real decision-making in Hong Kong, the 5% DTA dividend rate will be denied and the full 10% statutory WHT imposed. Hong Kong ESR requirements, where applicable, also demand adequate staff and premises in Hong Kong for the relevant activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Sham WFOE lacking economic substance

Statutory sanction: PRC EIT Law Art. 47 permits recharacterisation of the entire arrangement and denial of treaty benefits; the State Taxation Administration may impute the foreign income as distribution subject to 10% dividend WHT; TCAL Art. 63 imposes 50%-500% evasion penalty plus daily 0.05% overdue interest; the entity will be blacklisted under TCAL Art. 44.

Director personal liability: Directors face piercing of the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Art. 20 where the WFOE was used as a mere instrumentality; personal assets may be reached for restitution of unpaid tax; travel bans and exit restrictions apply; criminal liability can arise under Criminal Law Art. 201 for deceptive tax filing, with imprisonment of up to 7 years, and Art. 205 for false invoice schemes.
Aggressive transfer pricing / profit shifting

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Art. 41 allows the tax bureau to re-determine arm's length income; Art. 48 imposes special tax adjustment and interest calculated at the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points; TCAL Art. 63 adds 50%-500% penalty; neglect to provide contemporaneous documentation triggers an additional penalty under TCAL and adverse inference of understatement.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial controllers who sign or direct the transfer pricing manipulation are personally at risk in the recovery proceeding; the authorities can pierce the veil under Company Law Art. 20 to recover the adjusted tax from personal assets; exit bans under TCAL Art. 44; criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Art. 201 may follow if the adjustment reveals intentional falsification, carrying imprisonment of up to 7 years.
Withholding tax evasion and fraudulent DTA relief claim

Statutory sanction: TCAL Art. 69 requires the withholding agent (the WFOE) to pay under-withheld tax and imposes a fine of 50%-300% of the uncollected amount; the dividend/interest/royalty is recharacterised as a gross payment subject to 10% statutory WHT; treaty relief is revoked with retroactive effect and daily late-payment interest is applied.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE responsible for withholding may be jointly liable for the unremitted tax and fines after corporate veil-piercing; personal bank accounts and travel privileges can be frozen and exit banned; use of forged instruments triggers Criminal Law Art. 208 (forgery of tax certificates), while intentional non-withholding can be prosecuted under Art. 201, leading to imprisonment for up to 7 years and financial penalties.
