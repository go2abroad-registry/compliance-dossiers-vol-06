Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a WFOE under the PRC Company Law (2023) or Foreign Investment Law, provided the business is outside the Negative List. The registered capital and contribution schedule are set out in the Articles; shareholders must contribute in full within five years of incorporation unless a shorter period is specified. No blocked escrow account is legally required. Foreign currency capital must be remitted through the bank that handles the foreign exchange registration and the capital account.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; official electronic filing and invoice systems are administered via the national Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform (e-Tax) and the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform at etax.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The PRC-incorporated WFOE is registered with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues a Business License bearing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); the USCC is the statutory Tax Identification Number/VAT identification code for the WFOE, and no separate TIN certificate is issued. Step 2: Within 30 days after the Business License is issued, the WFOE must complete tax registration information confirmation and supplement through the e-Tax portal, including legal representative and financial officer real-name authentication, registered address, banking details, and capital structure; the China tax authority receives the business registration data directly through the one-license-one-code data sharing platform. Step 3: The WFOE must file for VAT identification and elect its VAT classification: general VAT taxpayer or small-scale VAT taxpayer; if annual taxable turnover exceeds CNY 5,000,000 or the WFOE intends to claim input VAT credits or export zero-rating, it must apply for general VAT taxpayer registration. Step 4: The WFOE must register for the electronic invoice service platform, obtain electronic invoice quotas, and integrate its billing/ERP system with the STA-approved Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) interface or use the web-based e-invoice module. Step 5: If the WFOE exports goods, it must separately register with China Customs via the China International Trade Single Window, obtain an operating customs registration code, and maintain the customs ten-digit code for export declarations. Step 6: A foreign non-resident entity such as the Hong Kong parent company is not issued a PRC USCC or VAT ID for the WFOE; a non-resident enterprise deriving PRC-sourced income must instead complete tax file number filing through the withholding agent or direct registration under the non-resident tax registration rules, and may separately apply for treaty benefits, before any PRC-source payments are remitted overseas.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. PRC law does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch pass-through election; the WFOE is inherently a resident enterprise subject to entity-level Enterprise Income Tax and is a separate VAT taxpayer. The only material election is the voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer before reaching the CNY 5,000,000 turnover threshold where the simplified small-scale taxpayer levy would otherwise apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) is the mandatory threshold for general VAT taxpayer registration; a WFOE below this threshold is ordinarily treated as a small-scale VAT taxpayer at the simplified levy rate, but may voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer to recover input VAT and access zero-rating.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical exports of goods, 0% VAT is available when the goods are loaded and exported from PRC customs territory under a valid Customs Export Declaration, the exporter and supplier are the same general VAT taxpayer, payment is received in convertible foreign currency from outside mainland China, and export proceeds are settled through a designated bank under State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules within the statutory deadline. For services, 0% VAT applies only if the WFOE supplies a service that falls within the STA's published Catalogue of Zero-rated Cross-border Taxable Services, the service is supplied to an overseas recipient, the service is wholly consumed outside mainland China, the contract, fund flow, invoice flow and any relevant documentation are consistent, and the WFOE keeps the substantiating export dossier for audit. Advertising, affiliate media-buying and lead-generation services are not automatically zero-rated; if classified as advertising services consumed abroad, they may instead qualify for VAT exemption rather than 0% zero-rating, which does not carry input VAT refund rights. To use 0%, the service arrangement should be documented as a qualifying business-process management or similar zero-rated service, if the factual substance supports that classification.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration / Bill of Entry) with customs release and export clearance data from the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill/cargo receipt evidencing physical export outside the PRC customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or SAFE-authorized bank export-proceeds collection and settlement record evidencing receipt of export consideration in freely convertible foreign currency
  • Signed cross-border service agreement and zero-rated service declaration (for qualifying services) evidencing an overseas recipient and consumption of the service outside mainland China

The WFOE must issue all VAT invoices through the STA-administered Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system or the legacy tax-controlled invoice management module integrated with the Golden Tax Phase IV system. Invoice data is transmitted to the tax authority in real time; each invoice receives a unique 20-digit invoice number, an anti-fraud QR code, and a tax-control digital seal before delivery to the buyer is permitted. High-volume entities must use the STA-certified API/interface for automated invoice creation, rejection, and red-letter reversal; manual paper invoices outside the tax-control system are not VAT-valid and do not support input VAT credit or export zero-rating.

The STA and China Customs use Golden Tax Phase IV to match customs export declarations, electronic VAT invoices, foreign-exchange settlement records and corporate tax returns. If the foreign-exchange consideration is not repatriated within 180 days, or if any document in the substantiation bundle is false, missing or internally inconsistent, the zero-rate is retrospectively reversed, the supply is recharacterized as domestic taxable supply subject to 6% VAT (or the applicable goods rate), and the taxpayer must repay any VAT refund/input credit previously taken plus late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day. Administrative penalties range from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid or refund fraudulently obtained; serious export-refund fraud carries criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law, suspension of refund eligibility, loss of general VAT taxpayer status, and customs credit-rating downgrade.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate system. The RMB is current-account convertible but capital account operations are still largely administered. For the WFOE, daily operating transactions such as payment for advertising services receive automatic current-account processing once the bank validates the underlying contract, tax filings and invoice. Dividends can be remitted outward after the annual audit confirms distributable profit, the corporate income tax has been settled, and a 5% withholding tax under the mainland-HK double tax arrangement has been paid; the bank will accept the remittance as a current account transaction without pre-SAFE approval. In contrast, all FDI injections into the WFOE are capital account items. They require a Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate, a designated capital account with a licensed bank, and confirmation of paid-in capital by a chartered auditor. Long-term outward direct investment from the WFOE likewise is subject to prior SAFE and MOFCOM registration. Conversion limits were historically tight, but the current regime allows settled funds to be kept in RMB without a mandatory surrender ratio. The enterprise must still comply with macro-prudential parameters, including the restriction that capital funds cannot be used for speculative securities investment or entrusted loans to related companies without prior approval. The overall convertibility framework provides operational freedom for trade and service-related flows while retaining robust administrative controls over the capital account to prevent short-term speculative pressure and maintain financial stability.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds, including service trade receipts from media buying, must be repatriated into the WFOE's domestic foreign exchange settlement account within 90 calendar days from the date of export service invoice issuance or customs declaration for physical goods. The current SAFE total-amount monitoring framework does not specify a fixed mandatory conversion ratio; an enterprise may retain foreign currency up to its SAFE-approved retention quota, which is typically based on the prior year's trade revenues. All amounts above that quota are mandatorily settled into RMB through the designated bank. No exporter is permitted to leave export proceeds outside the PRC unless a specific SAFE relaxation has been granted for an approved offshore account. Each receipt must be matched to the corresponding export declaration or service trade invoice in the bank's monitoring module within 30 days of receipt, and unmatched entries are flagged as regulatory exceptions.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Mercury, Wise and Revolut Business are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to open bank settlement accounts or to conduct foreign exchange settlement for a China-registered WFOE. The WFOE must maintain its primary operational and capital accounts with an onshore licensed foreign-exchange designated bank. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must wire registered capital via SWIFT MT103 or via CIPS for RMB injection into the WFOE's SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account. The SWIFT field 70 must carry the exact FDI purpose code and the SAFE FDI registration number. The receiving onshore bank, after verifying the remittance against the company's subscribed capital and business scope, will complete the SAFE admissions entry in the digital foreign exchange system and release the funds for conversion. Capital verification is performed either by the bank under the capital settlement under authenticity procedure or by a Chinese CPA who issues a formal capital verification report confirming the paid-in capital. The RMB funds resulting from settlement are credited to the operational RMB account and must be spent strictly in accordance with the WFOE's business scope; no speculative investments or unauthorized lending are permitted.Digital advertising and media-buying disbursements are service trade payments and should be executed via SWIFT MT103 with the correct SAFE service trade purpose code for advertising and media services, such as 222020. The bank requires a service trade foreign exchange payment application form, the underlying contract, the invoice from the foreign payee, proof of withholding tax payment or tax clearance, and an internal compliance check that the beneficiary is not sanctioned. For physical-goods campaigns involving promotional merchandise, a commercial letter of credit or documentary collection can be routed through the bank's trade finance desk, but the standard rail for intangible media, affiliate commissions and lead generation fees remains SWIFT MT103. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Mercury, Wise and Revolut Business are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to open bank settlement accounts or to conduct foreign exchange settlement for a China-registered WFOE. Their balances are not recognized as legal deposits for Chinese regulatory purposes and cannot be used for SAFE foreign exchange registration, capital settlement, tax filing or regulatory reporting. The WFOE must maintain its primary operational and capital accounts with an onshore licensed foreign-exchange designated bank. EMIs may at best serve as supplementary payment instruments for minor operating expenses, but their inability to handle FDI purpose codes, SAFE filings and tax withholding makes them legally and operationally non-viable as the main clearing infrastructure.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: All Hong Kong private companies limited by shares are required by Section 371 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) to prepare financial statements that give a true and fair view and to have those statements audited by a certified public accountant, unless the company is eligible for and validly claims audit exemption under Part 9. A private company (and its group, if applicable) is exempt for a financial year if it satisfies at least two of the following three criteria as at the balance sheet date: annual revenue not exceeding HKD 20 million; total assets not exceeding HKD 10 million; number of employees not exceeding 50. The exemption does not apply to companies whose constitutional documents, members, or shareholders require an audit, nor to companies carrying on regulated activities such as banking, insurance, securities, or money service operator activities. The audit threshold is therefore a reverse test: audit is compulsory unless the quantitative small-company exemption is available and a Section 356 resolution (or relevant exempt certificate) has been properly passed and filed with the Companies Registry.
