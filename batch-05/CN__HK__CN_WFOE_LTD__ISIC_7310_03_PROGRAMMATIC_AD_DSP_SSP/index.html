A pure virtual office, PO-box, or commercial mail-forwarding address is not permissible as the sole registered office for a PRC WFOE engaged in ISIC 7310 programmatic advertising and SSP operations. None. No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a WFOE established in the permitted advertising-services sector. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the Electronic Tax Bureau portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, and the Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong SAR (IRD) via the eTax portal at www.ird.gov.hk for Hong Kong-source profits or filings of any Hong Kong affiliate

E-government filing portal

The WFOE is incorporated with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that serves as the taxpayer identification number for PRC tax purposes. Immediately after incorporation, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the local STA office through the Electronic Tax Bureau, binding the USCC to the tax dossier, electing VAT taxpayer status, and registering for corporate income tax, VAT, stamp duty, and urban maintenance and construction tax. For foreign non-resident parent entities with no permanent establishment in mainland China, any PRC-source income is subject to withholding tax at the statutory rate and requires a withholding agent registration under the same USCC/TIN framework; the non-resident must obtain a certificate of tax residence from its home jurisdiction for treaty relief. In Hong Kong, a non-resident company deriving Hong Kong-source profits is treated by the IRD under the territorial source principle and must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance; there is no separate VAT/GST registration in Hong Kong. The WFOE should also ensure any Hong Kong affiliate maintains an active IRD taxpayer file and prepares the offshore profits claim documentation if profits are derived entirely outside Hong Kong.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT law, goods exported by the WFOE are zero-rated and eligible for VAT export rebate upon full customs exportation. For programmatic advertising, DSP/SSP, and other cross-border services under ISIC 7310, zero-rating or VAT exemption is available under the cross-border taxable activity rules for services that are supplied to overseas entities and consumed entirely outside mainland China; the WFOE must prove the service is rendered to a non-resident customer, the benefit of the service arises outside mainland China, and consideration is received in foreign exchange. The Hong Kong offshore profits claim follows the territorial source principle, requiring the Hong Kong entity, if any, to substantiate that profits arise from contracts concluded and services performed outside Hong Kong to qualify for non-taxation in Hong Kong.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export customs declaration with customs seal or electronic release), Bill of Lading or equivalent air waybill or master transport document, Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), foreign trade contract, commercial invoice, packing list, and product authenticity proof for goods export; for cross-border digital advertising services, the bundle must include the signed offshore service agreement, delivery and service acceptance evidence, payment confirmation through SWIFT or ACH rails, bank inward remittance certificate, and a declaration of offshore consumption
  • Cross-border service export substantiation: no physical customs declaration is required, but the taxpayer must retain electronic records showing the recipient's non-resident status and the offshore location of the advertising inventory or SSP/DSP services

Mainland China mandates electronic invoicing through the national Electronic Invoice Service Platform, with all VAT special invoices and general invoices subject to real-time transmission to the State Taxation Administration, digital stamping, tax-control QR codes, and integration with the WFOE's Unified Social Credit Code. The fully digital electronic invoice regime requires API-based issuance, automatic clearance against registered tax rates, and immediate invoice authenticity verification through the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform. Hong Kong does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoice mandate; invoices are governed by the Inland Revenue Ordinance record-keeping requirements and the Business Registration Ordinance.

The State Taxation Administration and customs authorities may audit export-rebate claims and cross-border zero-rated service filings. Failure to satisfy the documentary, customs-clearance, or foreign-exchange-repatriation conditions triggers disallowance of the zero-rating, forced VAT recapture, clawback of any refunded output tax, and imposition of interest. The PRC Tax Collection Administration Law further authorizes late payment surcharges at 0.05% per day, penalties from 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, and administrative fines; fraudulent export refund claims can lead to criminal liability for tax evasion. In Hong Kong, incorrect offshore profit claims may result in additional assessments, penalties of up to 300% of the tax understated, and prosecution under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed current-account convertibility framework under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations (2008) and the PBOC/SAFE implementing circulars. RMB is convertible for bona fide trade in goods and services, including programmatic advertising technology fees, once banks verify underlying contracts, invoices and three-party service relationship. Capital-account items, including inbound equity injections from the Hong Kong parent to the WFOE, require prior FDI registration with SAFE in the Capital Project Information System, followed by establishment of a dedicated foreign-currency capital account at a domestic bank. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong shareholder are treated as current-account dividends and are legally remittable after annual audit, but the bank must review the audited financial statements, China-corporate income tax settlement/payment proof, board dividend resolution, and AML beneficial-ownership confirmation. Surrender or mandatory conversion does not apply uniformly to current-account receipts, but export proceeds must be repatriated and, if held in a settlement account, subject to retention quotas. For capital inflows, the foreign currency must be converted to CNY through the bank at market rate and used within the approved expenditure scope. Therefore, while not a fully convertible jurisdiction, a WFOE can execute repatriation and settlement with rigorous documentary support.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE requires export foreign-exchange proceeds to be repatriated to the domestic delegated bank's Forex settlement account within the earlier of the trade-maturity date set in the sales contract or the statutory 90-day default from the performance/shipment date; banks are then obligated to credit the exporter and apply verification in the SAFE monitoring system. The foreign-currency settlement account retention quota is computed by the bank based on the exporter's verified current-account receipts from the previous 12 months. Any amount above that approved quota must be sold to the designated bank at the spot market rate, effectively a 100% mandatory surrender of the surplus and conversion into CNY. No fixed proportional surrender applies to the retained quota, but the exporter may voluntarily convert all or part.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the policy direction of the People's Bank of China (PBOC), is the operative central bank and FX administration authority for the mainland-incorporated WFOE; the Hong Kong SAR parent operates under the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) with no currency-control restrictions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent wires equity consideration through its licensed HK bank using SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the WFOE's dedicated foreign-currency capital account at the selected onshore bank. Prior to remittance, the WFOE must complete SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) registration and obtain the SAFE Capital Project identification number, which the receiving bank will verify in the Capital Project Information System. The remittance reference field must contain 'WFOE CAPITAL INJECTION - SAFE FDI REGISTRATION NO. [NUMBER]' and match the SAFE transaction purpose code for inbound direct-investment equity contribution. Upon receipt, the onshore bank converts the HKD/USD to CNY only for approved expenditure categories, and a Chinese certified public accountant issues the statutory capital-verification report which is subsequently filed with the local Market Supervision Administration / SAMR. No disbursement from the capital account occurs before KYC review, SAFE matching, and FX-conversion purpose compliance have been fully completed.For domestic operating costs such as payroll, server hosting, tax and local vendor services, the WFOE should use CNY disbursements through CNAPS from its basic deposit account. For cross-border service fees owed to foreign publishers, advertisers, SSP partners or data vendors, SWIFT MT103 is the appropriate clearing rail, supported by the underlying service contract, itemized invoice and SAFE current-account transaction declaration. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are structurally unsuitable for pure digital advertising services where settlement events are data-driven and recurring; standby LCs or bank payment undertakings may be used only where payment certainty is contractually required. Cross-border RMB settlement can be routed via CIPS through the same relationship bank, reducing USD conversion cost and correspondent-bank latency, and every transfer must be tagged with the relevant current-account FX purpose code.Onshore deliverable FX forward contracts with genuine underlying current-account or capital-account exposure, concluded with a licensed domestic bank under ISDA/NAFMII documentation and verified by SAFE exposure reviews.Onshore FX swaps and cross-currency swaps used to hedge real operational, dividend or intercompany loan exposures, subject to SAFE registration and bank-supported risk exposure validation.Offshore NDFs are generally not permitted for the mainland WFOE; only the Hong Kong parent may access offshore CNH NDF markets, and the onshore entity must limit derivatives to fully deliverable instruments with genuine trade or servicing exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Onshore Chinese banking law and PBOC/SAFE licensing require a licensed domestic banking institution with access to CNAPS and the SAFE Capital Project Information System. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut, Payoneer, and Currenxie are neither licensed nor regulated as depository institutions in mainland China and cannot hold a WFOE's CNY operating accounts, receive FDI capital-account remittances, perform statutory tax or SAFE reporting, or support a CPA capital-verification report. Their practical utility is confined to ancillary fund flows of the Hong Kong holding entity in non-CNY corridors and they cannot replace domestic bank rails.
