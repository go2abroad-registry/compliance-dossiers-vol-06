Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-tax.chinatax.gov.cn). The State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is also the WFOE's statutory taxpayer identification number.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign (including Hong Kong) investor notarizes/legalizes its incorporation documents and board resolutions; if the investor is a Hong Kong company, mainland registration normally requires a notarized certificate of good standing and certified copies of the Hong Kong business registration. Step 2: File the WFOE business license application with SAMR under the PRC Company Law and the Foreign Investment Law; after approval, SAMR issues the 18-digit USCC printed on the business license. Step 3: The USCC is automatically transmitted to the STA through the one-window registration system; the WFOE becomes subject to full tax registration without a separate tax registration certificate. Step 4: Within 30 days after the business license is issued, complete the taxpayer information confirmation, real-name registration for legal representative/finance officer, bank account filing, accounting policy filing, and tax collector assignment at the competent local STA branch. Step 5: Select VAT taxpayer identity: small-scale taxpayer (3% collection rate, eligible for applicable SME exemptions) or general VAT taxpayer (required for meaningful input VAT credits and export zero-rating). Step 6: Through the Electronic Tax Bureau, apply for invoice issuance rights, digital invoice quota, and a tax-control UKey for the Full Digital Electronic Invoice/FDEI platform. Step 7: If the foreign non-resident parent derives PRC-source income (dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees), the WFOE must complete non-resident enterprise tax registration and contract withholding record filing before outward remittance, and the parent obtains a non-resident tax identification/code from the STA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not permit check-the-box, pass-through, or hybrid mismatch entity classification elections; a WFOE is automatically a resident enterprise subject to entity-level Enterprise Income Tax at the statutory 25% rate. Preferential EIT rates for small low-profit enterprises and high-tech enterprises are administrative concessions, not entity classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for basic VAT registration: every incorporated entity must register upon issuance of the USCC. A cumulative taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 in any trailing 12-month period triggers compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration; registered small-scale taxpayers below this threshold may use the simplified collection rate (currently 3%, with statutory SME exemptions for monthly sales up to RMB 100,000 where applicable).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: a general VAT taxpayer exporting goods may claim 0% VAT if the goods physically leave China under a valid Customs Export Declaration, title passes to the overseas buyer, and the export proceeds are repatriated and verified in foreign exchange. Qualifying offshore services: cross-border digital and modern services supplied to an overseas customer and wholly consumed outside China are zero-rated if they fall within the statutory zero-rating catalogue (including software, IT, R&D, design, and offshore outsourcing services), or VAT-exempt under the cross-border services rules if the service is not zero-rated; ASO/SEO/multilingual localization services must be documented as delivered to the overseas principal, produced without onshore consumption by PRC residents, and disconnected from PRC real property or goods. Services connected to a PRC recipient, PRC resident user, or PRC-located asset remain taxable at 6%.

  • China Customs Export Declaration (export goods declaration) with customs release endorsement, filed through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Original Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical exportation and overseas destination
  • Bank-issued Foreign Exchange Income Declaration Slip or SAFE online monitoring record proving export proceeds repatriation within the statutory period
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export/offshore service contract
  • For services: executed cross-border agreement, service acceptance certificate, performance/delivery evidence proving offshore consumption, and foreign currency receipt records
  • Copy of the zero-rated export VAT invoice or FDEI issued for the cross-border supply

The STA operates the Golden Tax System Phase IV and the nationwide Full Digital Electronic Invoice (FDEI) platform. All VAT taxpayers must issue invoices through the Electronic Tax Bureau, a tax-control UKey, or an authorized FDEI application programming interface (API). Each FDEI is assigned a unique nationwide invoice number and encrypted QR code, uploaded in near real time for central clearance, reconciliation, and anti-fraud risk control. Paper invoices are being phased out; every invoice must match the VAT return, and for exports each invoice must be reconcilable with the Customs Export Declaration and foreign-exchange receipt record.

Export zero-rating claims are cross-checked by STA against customs export data, SAFE foreign-exchange receipts, invoice data, and bank settlement records. If documentation is incomplete, false, or the proceeds are not repatriated by the deadline, STA can claw back the zero-rated treatment, assess the VAT due at 6% (or 13% if goods), impose late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export VAT schemes are criminally prosecutable under Criminal Law Article 204, with fines, imprisonment, and up to life imprisonment where the amount is especially huge. Administrative consequences include downgrade of the taxpayer credit rating, suspension of export tax refund/zero-rating eligibility, and inclusion in the serious untrustworthy taxpayer blacklist.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains current-account convertibility for genuine trade and service transactions, but capital-account items remain restricted. RMB is not freely convertible outside the mainland; offshore CNH is a separate pool. All onshore FX conversion must be conducted through licensed banks that apply PBOC/SAFE genuine-transaction checks. For an ASO/SEO localization agency, service-export fees may be received in FX and either retained or converted into RMB, but banks require contracts, invoices, delivery evidence, tax declarations, and entry into SAFE's cross-border monitoring platform. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is legally permissible after closing-year audit, tax clearance and withholding; the Mainland-Hong Kong tax arrangement may reduce dividend withholding to 5% if the HK parent satisfies the 25% shareholding and beneficial-ownership conditions. Hurdles include strict capital-account filing under SAFE, on-site bank verification of beneficial owners, limited use of unlicensed fintech EMIs, slower settlement windows, and continued AML scrutiny on cross-border digital services. The WFOE cannot use a Hong Kong EMI as a substitute for capital conversion or tax repatriation; funds must flow through a licensed PRC bank using proper settlement codes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE current-account policy there is no mandatory percentage surrender of export proceeds; the statutory requirement is timely repatriation and bank-verified settlement. For goods exports, bank verification must occur within 180 days of the customs declaration date or the contractual settlement date. For service exports relevant to ISIC 7310, receipts must be repatriated within 180 days after service completion or payment due date and reported on SAFE's monitoring platform. Conversion ratio is voluntary from 0% to 100%; an eligible receipt may be fully retained as FX or fully converted to RMB at onshore spot rate through a licensed bank.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

They can serve only as ancillary offshore collection/payment tools for low-risk operational amounts, and each transaction must be fully reconciled to invoices and reflected in PRC corporate bank statements. They must not be used for inbound FDI capital injection, dividend remittance, SAFE reporting, or tax payments.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) sections 359-369, a private company is exempt from statutory audit only if it qualifies as a 'small private company' for the financial year, meaning it meets ALL of the following conditions: (a) annual revenue not exceeding HKD 20,000,000 (approximately USD 2,564,000); (b) total assets not exceeding HKD 20,000,000 (approximately USD 2,564,000); and (c) total employees (including directors) not exceeding 50. If any of these thresholds is exceeded, the company must undergo an independent audit by a practising certified public accountant. Additionally, audit exemption is unavailable to public companies, companies within a group unless consolidated group thresholds are also met, financial institutions, insurance companies, securities firms, or charitable institutions, regardless of size. Companies exempted from audit must still file unaudited financial statements and a directors' declaration (in accordance with section 360) together with their annual return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: In RMB, under SAT Announcement No. 42 (2016): Local File required if tangible property related-party transactions equal or exceed RMB 200 million; intangible property transfers equal or exceed RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers equal or exceed RMB 100 million; related-party services equal or exceed RMB 100 million; or other related-party transactions equal or exceed RMB 100 million. A Master File is additionally required when annual total related-party transactions of the WFOE exceed RMB 1 billion and the MNE group revenue threshold in the Announcement is satisfied. Country-by-Country Reporting applies where group consolidated revenue for the prior year reached RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: Chinese tax authorities apply the substance-over-form doctrine and the OECD beneficial ownership analysis. The Chinese WFOE must demonstrate its own Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for ASO and SEO localization, including contracting with app publishers, employing local keyword and content specialists, controlling marketing strategy, maintaining operational management, and managing billing and bank accounts. A Hong Kong shareholder structured purely as a passive shelf company will fail the beneficial ownership test and treaty relief will be denied. In Hong Kong, the Economic Substance Regulations (Cap. 615) impose substance requirements on relevant geographically mobile activities, but regardless of ESR scope, the Hong Kong entity must have sufficient board management, employees, records, and decision-making substance to satisfy mainland Chinese tax authorities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Cross-Border Revenue Concealment

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax; daily late payment interest of 0.05% under Article 32; PRC Criminal Law Article 201: criminal tax evasion punishable by fines up to 500% of the evaded amount and fixed-term imprisonment of up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Under the PRC Company Law, the corporate veil may be pierced where directors or shareholders abuse limited liability to evade tax or strip corporate assets. Directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, penalties, and interest. The legal representative may be blacklisted as a Serious Untrustworthy Taxpayer, triggering travel bans, denial of exit from China, asset freezes, and criminal imprisonment upon conviction.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 48 authorize special tax adjustments, recalculated taxable income, and compensating interest from the transaction year. If fraudulent pricing is shown, a 50% to 500% penalty may be imposed under the Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63. False invoicing in connection with the arrangement triggers Criminal Law Article 205 with fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve fictional service agreements, backdate transfer pricing documentation, or sign false beneficial ownership certificates may be personally fined and criminally prosecuted. In insolvency, directors may be ordered to contribute personal assets to satisfy tax debts when they breached fiduciary duties. Hong Kong directors who make false statements to the tax authorities may additionally face prosecution and travel restrictions.
Shell Company, Treaty Abuse, and AML Disregard

Statutory sanction: Treaty benefits are denied under the Mainland China-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement beneficial ownership requirement, SAT Announcement No. 15 (2015), and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47. The distribution is recharacterized and the statutory 10% withholding tax applies, with interest and penalties. In Hong Kong, the Inland Revenue Ordinance sections 61, 61A, and 82A permit the Inland Revenue Department to disregard artificial transactions, assess additional tax, and prosecute tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners who sign false beneficial ownership declarations may be criminally prosecuted for tax evasion in mainland China and for making false statements to a Hong Kong public officer, exposing them to imprisonment. Civil asset restitution may be ordered against directors where treaty benefits were received by a shell and the corporate form was abused. Both jurisdictions may impose blacklisting and travel bans on the responsible individuals.
