Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic registrations, filings and invoice controls are performed through the STA's Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) web portal and the STA's official website www.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

After the WFOE obtains its business license from the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR), the legal representative or authorized tax agent must, within 30 calendar days, complete taxpayer status establishment with the STA sub-office at the registered address in China. The 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by AMR constitutes the enterprise TIN. For VAT, the WFOE must file a taxpayer type registration (small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer) through the Electronic Tax Bureau, attaching the USCC, business license, articles of association, registered-address proof, and responsible-person identity documents. The STA then activates the e-tax account and issues the electronic tax registration record. A foreign non-resident entity with no Chinese place of business that derives China-source income must separately register for a non-resident enterprise TIN at the STA office of the Chinese withholding agent, supplying overseas incorporation documents, the underlying contract, and responsible-person identification, and is then enabled to perform withholding and reverse-charge VAT filing.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is not fully convertible on the capital account. The current account is effectively convertible, meaning fees and trade-related payments for a public relations and communication company can be remitted after a genuine transaction review by a designated bank. Capital account transactions, including equity capital contributions, overseas loans, cross-border guarantees, and dividend repatriation, are separately controlled by SAFE and require registration, validation, and in certain cases approval. For this Hong Kong-owned WFOE, the main FX friction points are the need to prove commercial substance behind every remittance, the annual tax settlement and withholding tax clearance before dividends can leave China, and the inability to use an offshore EMI as a substitute onshore clearing bank. There is no sweeping capital account convertibility; every foreign exchange receipt or payment must be matched to a real underlying transaction and reported under the international receipts and payments reporting system. Profit remittance is therefore feasible but requires audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement, withholding tax certificate, and SAFE transaction documentation. The practical effect is that liquidity management must be planned around documentary lead times, not immediate fungibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE practice, export proceeds no longer need to be mandatorily converted into RMB. The statutory requirement is repatriation: goods-trade foreign exchange receipts must be brought back onshore and settled through the bank's trade foreign exchange supervision electronic system, generally within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration, unless a deferred payment exception is approved by SAFE. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent; a PRC enterprise may retain 100% of export proceeds in a current-account foreign exchange account or convert at any time to RMB via a designated bank, but the underlying trade details, customs data, and logistics documents must support the receipt. Any export surpluses not repatriated within the allowed period are subject to reported violations and enforcement action under PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central monetary authority, with delegated administration and enforcement of exchange controls through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable for the onshore WFOE. Foreign or Hong Kong-licensed e-money institutions and digital EMI platforms do not hold the PRC Payment Business License nor the SAFE banking license required to operate a domestic settlement account for a PRC company. They cannot undertake SAFE capital account validation, tax withholding, invoice issuance, customs/trade verification, or RMB clearing through China's national clearing infrastructure. Using an offshore EMI as the primary treasury rail for the WFOE would breach PBOC Administrative Measures for Non-Financial Payment Institutions, the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, and the negative-list restrictions on non-bank financial services. An offshore EMI may only serve as a non-core collection proxy for the Hong Kong parent before lawful remittance into China. Feasibility rating: Not viable for the WFOE; low supplementary viability at the Hong Kong shareholder level only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must remit registered capital via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's SAFE-designated foreign exchange capital account at the selected onshore bank. The remittance instruction must include the SAFE FDI registration code and remittance narration showing 'paid-in registered capital contribution' and be classified under the SAFE Balance of Payments code for direct investment equity in China. The designated bank will post the receipt on SAFE's Foreign Exchange Administrative Platform. After the funds arrive, a PRC-registered accounting firm must issue a capital verification report; the paid-in capital is then reported through the Foreign Investment Comprehensive Reporting System and National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System. The capital account funds cannot be disbursed for operating purposes until each planned use is declared with supporting contracts, invoices, and any required tax payment certificates, and the funds are settled into the WFOE's RMB account.For recurring overseas PR retainers and media purchases, the standard clearing rail is a direct SWIFT MT103 wire made after the bank receives a service trade outward remittance application, the underlying media contract and invoice, proof of media delivery or performance, and the PRC tax withholding certificate or tax clearance. Commercial Letters of Credit are available but are not needed for ordinary retainers; they may be used for high-value media procurement or event sponsorship where an independent bank undertaking is required. Documentary Collections can be used for settlement of approved media-vendor invoices against delivery documents, but the low-risk nature of PR agency services means documentary collections are infrequent. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required when annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; or RMB 40 million for related-party service and other transactions. Master File is required when the WFOE must prepare a Local File and the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue exceeded RMB 5 billion in the prior year. Related-party transaction forms in the G100000 Series must be filed with the annual CIT return whenever related-party transactions exist.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China applies general anti-avoidance rules under EIT Law Article 47 and the beneficial-ownership rules in SAT Public Notice 2018 No. 9, requiring the HK shareholder to demonstrate real Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong. Hong Kong's foreign-source income exemption and economic substance requirements also require adequate staff, premises and active business decision-making in Hong Kong for the HK parent to preserve treaty benefits. A shell parent with no CIGA in Hong Kong will be denied treaty relief in China, recharacterised as a conduit, and subjected to withholding tax, special tax adjustment and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 requires payment of the shortfall plus a penalty of 50%-500% of unpaid tax; Article 32 adds a daily late-payment surcharge. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes fines and imprisonment up to 7 years where the evasion amount is large and exceeds the statutory ratio of taxable income.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false filings are treated as directly responsible persons. Courts may pierce the corporate veil and impose joint liability for unpaid tax and penalties where the company is stripped of assets, freeze personal assets, preclude travel under the tax blacklist, and impose criminal imprisonment up to 7 years.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Failure / Special Tax Adjustment

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41, 44 and 48 allow recharacterisation and special tax adjustment. SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42 requires contemporaneous documentation; failure to file forms attracts penalties under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 62, and interest on the adjusted tax is imposed at the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points per year where documentation is missing. Fraudulent TP schemes attract Article 63 penalties of 50%-500% and Criminal Law Article 201 liability.

Director personal liability: If the transfer-pricing adjustment arises from a deliberate scheme benefiting the HK shareholder and the WFOE is left insolvent, directors and controlling persons may be jointly liable for adjusted tax and penalties, placed on the serious tax-dishonesty blacklist with exit denial, and prosecuted criminally where false documentation was knowingly used.
Withholding Tax Evasion / Treaty Abuse / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 makes a withholding agent that fails to withhold liable for the unpaid tax plus a penalty of 50%-300% of the unpaid amount; Article 32 adds late-payment surcharge. EIT Law Articles 37 and 47 and SAT Public Notice 2018 No. 9 permit denial of treaty benefits and recharacterisation, with interest and penalties. Criminal Law Article 201 applies to intentional evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign dividend resolutions and withholding returns are personally exposed to restitution of unpaid taxes, freezing of personal bank assets in China, publication of tax violations and travel bans. If the structure is a sham and funds are diverted for personal use, criminal liability may result in imprisonment from 3 to 10 years.
