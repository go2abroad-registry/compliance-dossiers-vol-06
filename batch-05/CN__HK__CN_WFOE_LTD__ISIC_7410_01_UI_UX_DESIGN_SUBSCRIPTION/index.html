Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; registration, filing, and e-invoice administration are performed through the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau portal and the fully digitalized e-invoice service platform. The WFOE must register with the STA's local competent tax office at the address on its mainland China business license. Hong Kong's Inland Revenue Department is not a competent tax authority for the mainland WFOE and only becomes relevant for the separate Hong Kong shareholder entity profits tax position.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the WFOE through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the PRC Business License, which contains the statutory 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The USCC is the WFOE's TIN for all PRC tax purposes and is required for VAT and Corporate Income Tax filings. Step 2: Complete tax registration with the local STA office through the Electronic Tax Bureau, generally within 30 days after the business license is issued; the STA will activate the WFOE's taxpayer profile for Corporate Income Tax, VAT, individual income tax withholding, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, and stamp duty. Step 3: Appoint the legal representative and financial/tax responsible officers, bind the entity's bank account, and activate the electronic tax filing identity and digital signature. Step 4: Apply for invoice issuance authorization, select the VAT taxpayer status (small-scale taxpayer or VAT general taxpayer), and obtain the e-invoice or tax-control registration required for issuing fully digitalized electronic invoices. Step 5: For a foreign/Hong Kong non-resident investor that creates no permanent establishment in mainland China, no separate PRC TIN is required; the WFOE discharges any PRC withholding obligations using its own USCC. If the non-resident investor does create a permanent establishment in China, it must separately complete non-resident taxpayer registration with the STA and obtain its own statutory TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise taxed at entity level under the PRC Corporate Income Tax Law on worldwide income. No pass-through treatment is available; the standard CIT rate is 25%, with reduced rates available only where the entity qualifies under statutory incentives such as small low-profit enterprise treatment or encouraged industry preferential rates.

Available election forms

None: China does not have a U.S.-style check-the-box election, hybrid mismatch election, or discretionary entity classification mechanism. The only elections available to the WFOE are administrative choices such as VAT small-scale taxpayer versus general taxpayer status and certain accounting/tax depreciation methods; these are not statutory entity classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (CNY 5 million) of annual taxable sales, excluding VAT, is the mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration threshold. If the WFOE's annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5 million, it must register as a VAT general taxpayer. If at or below RMB 5 million, it may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer subject to the simplified levy rate currently 3%, subject to temporary reductions. For the WFOE's UI/UX design and digital design subscription services supplied domestically, the applicable output VAT rate is 6% for a VAT general taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For PRC VAT purposes, Hong Kong and other non-mainland territories are treated as outside mainland China. The WFOE may claim 0% VAT on its UI/UX design and digital design subscription services sold to an overseas or Hong Kong customer only if the service is an enumerated zero-rated cross-border taxable service, design services being enumerated, and all of the following conditions are satisfied: (i) the customer is located outside mainland China; (ii) the service is wholly consumed outside mainland China; (iii) the WFOE receives consideration in foreign currency through a designated bank and records the receipt under SAFE foreign exchange administration rules; (iv) the WFOE is registered as a VAT general taxpayer; (v) the WFOE maintains a complete export service dossier evidencing that the service is not used, in whole or in part, within mainland China; and (vi) the WFOE files the required cross-border service transaction declaration in the Electronic Tax Bureau. Where zero-rating applies, output VAT is 0% and associated input VAT may be refunded or offset. Pure cross-border design services do not require customs clearance; customs documentation is required only if the service is bundled with physical goods exported from China.

  • Export Service Agreement / Statement of Work between the WFOE and the overseas customer, clearly identifying the design subscription service, delivery milestones, contract value, and invoicing schedule
  • Commercial invoice issued through the fully digitalized e-invoice system showing the export service nature and 0% VAT treatment
  • Foreign exchange receipt evidence, including the bank settlement voucher, SAFE foreign exchange receipt record, or Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate equivalent, proving receipt and repatriation of payment for the exported services
  • Service delivery and acceptance evidence, such as UI/UX design files, access logs, delivery email chains, or signed offshore customer acceptance certificate proving consumption outside mainland China
  • Cross-border service transaction declaration/registration record submitted through the STA/SAFE systems, including the applicable service transaction code
  • If physical deliverables are exported through customs: Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry), Bill of Lading or Airway Bill, packing list, and customs inspection records

The WFOE must issue invoices through the STA's New VAT Invoice Management System and, where activated, the Fully Digitalized E-Invoice platform. Invoice data is uploaded and cleared in real time through the Golden Tax Phase IV system. Every invoice must carry a unique invoice code, anti-counterfeiting QR stamp, and an electronic signature generated by the tax-control device or approved API interface. The fiscal clearance verification matches the seller's USCC, buyer's USCC or identifier, invoice amount, applicable VAT rate, and invoice type before the invoice is delivered to the buyer; abnormal or high-risk invoices are automatically flagged or blocked by the STA.

The STA and SAFE cross-match e-invoice data, foreign exchange receipts, cross-border service declarations, and Corporate Income Tax filings under the Golden Tax Phase IV platform. If a 0% export VAT claim is fraudulent, insufficiently documented, or cannot be substantiated, the STA may retrospectively claw back the output VAT at the applicable 6% rate, impose late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day on the unpaid tax, and levy administrative fines ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid. Serious cases involving forged export documentation or fraudulent zero-rating/export refund claims may trigger criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law and prospective revocation of the WFOE's right to zero-rating or export refund treatment. Under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law, where fraud or evasion is established, there is no statutory limitation period for tax recovery, allowing unrestricted retrospective clawback.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII, supported by PBOC and SAFE administrative regulations, while capital-account transactions remain registration-and-approval oriented. For the WFOE, inbound foreign direct investment is classified as a capital-account inflow and requires FDI registration, a local bank capital account, and a post-receipt FX settlement process. The registered capital is quoted in RMB but may be paid in foreign currency at the prevailing market rate; once converted through the capital-account FX settlement mechanism, the RMB enters a dedicated settlement account and can be deployed only within the approved business scope after SAFE and the bank verify authenticity. Export proceeds from UI/UX design subscriptions and related services, if received in foreign currency, must be repatriated into an onshore current-account FX account within the statutory collection window and may be either retained in foreign exchange or converted into RMB at a designated bank. Dividend remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permitted after audited financial statements confirm no accumulated losses and the local tax authority clears the enterprise income tax and dividend withholding; the remittance is treated as current-account repatriation but triggers bank-level documentary authenticity review. The practical hurdles are stringent supporting-document standards for intangible service exports, anti-circumvention scrutiny of recurring subscription flows, and capital deployment monitoring under the registered FDI capital account. Any subsequent increase in paid-in capital, debt-to-equity swap, or liquidation repatriation requires a new SAFE registration and potentially a tax settlement certificate. Overall, the regime is partially convertible: operational and trade-related FX is highly liquid, while capital-related FX remains document-bound and time-sensitive in approval and verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory collection window: 180 calendar days from the date of the corresponding export or service performance declaration; if the commercial contract provides for deferred receipt beyond 180 days, a SAFE extension filing must be made before the statutory deadline. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% under the current PBOC/SAFE reform framework; PRC law does not require surrender. The exporter may retain the full amount in a current-account foreign-currency bank account or voluntarily sell it to a designated PRC bank at market rates, but all export receipts must be repatriated onshore unless SAFE has pre-approved offshore retention.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange of the People's Republic of China (SAFE), subsidiary executive agency of the People's Bank of China (PBOC) under the State Council regulatory framework

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Wise and Stripe cannot operate as licensed banks in China and are legally barred from holding domestic RMB accounts or performing primary settlement for a PRC WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs may be feasible only as auxiliary collection or payment gateways for low-value international design subscriptions, and then only through a licensed cross-border third-party payment institution (TPPI) under SAFE pilot rules, such as an approved payment company integrated with the bank's foreign exchange reporting. For the treasury core, a domestic tier-1 commercial bank is mandatory because EMIs cannot satisfy PBOC anti-money-laundering requirements No. 1, No. 3, and No. 85, and cannot issue a capital verification report or support SAFE FDI registration. Feasibility rating: Low as the sole treasury delivery mechanism; Medium as an auxiliary collection gateway when layered with an onshore bank and a licensed TPPI.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound FDI wires must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the Hong Kong parent or ultimate investor to the WFOE-specific SAFE-registered capital account at the designated PRC bank. The beneficiary name must exactly match the business license English name, the remitter must reflect the investing entity, and the remittance narrative must carry Purpose Code FDI-101 Equity Subscription together with the contract or share subscription reference. The receiving bank will verify the SAFE foreign direct investment registration certificate, record the transaction in the FDIS/FDI system, and require the enterprise to complete the Foreign Exchange Capital Settlement Application before any RMB conversion. After the bank releases the RMB into the domestic settlement account, a PRC CPA must issue a formal capital verification report; subsequent usage of the capital account proceeds is supervised by the bank and SAFE and confined to the approved operating purposes, including payroll, rent, software tools, third-party design subcontracting, and tax obligations.Design service trade settlements use SWIFT MT103 with the appropriate SAFE purpose code, typically royalty and fee payments, supported by the service agreement, an invoice, a deliverable acceptance certificate, and internal authenticity file. For physical asset procurement or high-value infrastructure purchases, a Commercial Letter of Credit under MT700 provides bank-backed payment assurance; Documentary Collections under MT400 or MT410 are acceptable for lower-risk, lower-value trade flows with established counterparties. Operational RMB disbursements inside China are cleared through CNAPS HVPS; cross-border RMB payments with overseas vendors should follow the RMB Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) rules when the counterparty accepts RMB, while all FX-denominated vendor payments remain subject to SWIFT and bank-level SAFE reporting.Onshore RMB forward contracts with a genuine underlying trade or investment exposure disclosed to the SAFE-authorized bank and effective through a master ISDA or ISDA-style FX derivative agreementFX swaps between CNY and USD for short-term liquidity management, provided the bank has counterparty credit approval and the enterprise maintains a realised underlying transactionCross-currency swaps for long-term capital and interest cash-flow hedging associated with the Hong Kong parent's inward fundingOffshore non-deliverable forward contracts (NDFs) executable through the Hong Kong parent's licensed treasury desk for CNH exposure protection, only where the underlying is demonstrable, and with no synthetic RMB activity that PBOC or SAFE would deem unlawfulPermitted currency options on a select bank-approved derivative licence volume, with the notional capped below the firm's aggregate projected FX exposure, requiring daily mark-to-market monitoring and monthly compliance reporting to the bank treasury

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Wise and Stripe cannot operate as licensed banks in China and are legally barred from holding domestic RMB accounts or performing primary settlement for a PRC WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs may be feasible only as auxiliary collection or payment gateways for low-value international design subscriptions, and then only through a licensed cross-border third-party payment institution (TPPI) under SAFE pilot rules, such as an approved payment company integrated with the bank's foreign exchange reporting. For the treasury core, a domestic tier-1 commercial bank is mandatory because EMIs cannot satisfy PBOC anti-money-laundering requirements No. 1, No. 3, and No. 85, and cannot issue a capital verification report or support SAFE FDI registration. Feasibility rating: Low as the sole treasury delivery mechanism; Medium as an auxiliary collection gateway when layered with an onshore bank and a licensed TPPI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File / contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is mandatory for the fiscal year if related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible-asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible-asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial-asset transfers; RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions including design services, management fees, interest and royalties; Master File is required when aggregate related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion or the multinational group satisfies the global group-income threshold under China's transfer-pricing rules

Economic substance scope: Mainland China has not enacted UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but economic-substance principles are embedded in the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47, the beneficial-ownership doctrine under SAT Public Notice 2015 No. 60 and Public Notice 2018 No. 9, and the Special Tax Adjustment provisions; the WFOE must have real staff, premises, decision-making and risk-bearing capacity for its design-subscription business; a Hong Kong parent company claiming DTA benefits must demonstrate core income-generating activities in Hong Kong under the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance and DIPN 48, otherwise the 5% dividend rate or reduced interest/royalty rates may be denied

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63: recovery of evaded tax, daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge and a fine of 0.5 to 5 times the evaded amount; Criminal Law Article 201: tax fraud involving more than RMB 100,000 of evaded tax and more than 10% of tax payable is punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years plus criminal fines, and amounts above RMB 500,000 and 30% of tax payable are punishable by imprisonment between 3 and 7 years plus fines

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or directed false returns are jointly and severally liable for restitution of evaded tax and fines; personal assets may be frozen and seized; directors may be placed on the tax exit-control blacklist under Tax Collection Law Article 44 and prohibited from leaving China; criminal conviction can result in imprisonment, disqualification from serving as directors in China and, for cross-border cases, extradition or mutual legal assistance exposure
Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Fraud and Tax-Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 General Anti-Avoidance Rule; the transaction may be recharacterised and treaty benefits denied under SAT Public Notice 2015 No. 60 and Public Notice 2018 No. 9; the statutory 10% withholding tax is then collected with late surcharge and a penalty of up to 50% of underpaid tax under Tax Collection Law; false treaty-benefit filings also constitute tax evasion and fraud under Criminal Law Article 201 and can trigger VAT invoice fraud liability under Article 205 if fictitious invoices are used

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implemented sham Hong Kong substance arrangements are jointly and severally liable for the under-withheld tax and penalties; personal assets in China and Hong Kong can be reached through mutual enforcement; directors face criminal investigation, travel bans and exit-control blacklisting; imprisonment of up to 7 years is possible for egregious tax fraud, and conviction may include confiscation of the economic benefit obtained through treaty abuse
AML / Illegal Payment Processing and Structuring Through Design-Subscription Platforms

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Article 191 Money Laundering: imprisonment up to 5 years, or 5 to 10 years for serious circumstances, plus fines and confiscation; the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes fines on institutions and responsible persons for failure to perform customer due diligence or report large and suspicious transactions; the People's Bank of China and SAFE can freeze accounts, revoke payment access and blacklist the company; tax authorities may additionally impose penalties under the Tax Collection Law where the concealment caused underpayment

Director personal liability: Directors as the responsible compliance officers face personal criminal prosecution if they directed the structuring or knew of the money-laundering purpose; civil asset restitution and confiscation of laundered funds apply; the directors' personal property may be attached; directors are subject to exit-control blacklisting and travel bans; imprisonment can extend to 10 years for serious money-laundering and up to 7 years for overlapping tax fraud, with both liabilities capable of being pursued cumulatively
