Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and its provincial, municipal, and county tax service offices; online registration and filing are administered through the State Taxation Administration electronic tax administration portal (e-Tax), which is the unified electronic taxpayer gateway for national tax registration, VAT declaration, Enterprise Income Tax filing, invoice administration, and export rebate applications.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Business License bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); the USCC is the statutory Tax Identification Number and VAT registration number. Step 2 - Within 30 calendar days of the Business License date, complete the initial tax registration through the e-Tax portal or in person at the competent tax service office; this creates the taxpayer file under the Tax Collection Administration Law and includes legal representative details, registered address, accounting standards, bank account information, and contact particulars. Step 3 - Submit the VAT taxpayer status filing so that the tax authority can classify the company as a general VAT taxpayer or a small-scale VAT taxpayer; the WFOE should apply for general taxpayer status if it needs to issue special VAT invoices, claim input VAT credits, or export with VAT zero-rating. Step 4 - Register the invoice issuance configuration in the e-Tax invoice system, including the invoice quota, invoice type, tax rate, and, where applicable, the full digital e-invoice capability. Step 5 - If the WFOE will export goods, complete China Customs registration through the Customs authorities to obtain an enterprise customs code and customs declaration right. Step 6 - For a foreign non-resident enterprise that is the shareholder, such as the Hong Kong parent, the parent does not obtain a TIN merely by setting up the WFOE; however, if the non-resident enterprise has China-source income without a permanent establishment, the non-resident or its withholding agent must complete the non-resident enterprise tax registration with the competent tax authority so that the non-resident taxpayer identification number can be allocated for withholding filing and treaty claims.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not have a check-the-box or pass-through election. A WFOE is a resident enterprise taxed at the entity level under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed through China's general anti-avoidance rules and special tax adjustment provisions rather than through an elective regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for basic VAT registration: every incorporated enterprise in the PRC must register for VAT upon obtaining its Business License. For general VAT taxpayer status, mandatory classification applies when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 (approximately USD 690,000); a new service-oriented WFOE may also voluntarily apply for general taxpayer status where it has fixed business premises, sound accounting, and a demonstrated need to issue special VAT invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for VAT zero-rating (and the associated export tax rebate) when they are physically exported from China and the customs declaration, logistics documents, export receivable, and foreign-currency receipt are matched to the same export transaction. Qualifying offshore services and digital assets, including licensed photographs, visual content, and other professional or cultural and creative services supplied to non-resident clients, qualify for zero-rating where the services are listed in the STA's cross-border zero-rate catalogue, the recipient is located outside mainland China, the service is performed or consumed entirely outside mainland China, the consideration is paid in foreign currency, and the proceeds are repatriated to China in compliance with the foreign exchange rules. The WFOE must also maintain contemporaneous evidence of the offshore nature of the transaction, including the contract, invoice at zero rate, payment records, and service-consumption indicators.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods customs bill of entry) for physical exports, showing the export commodity code, declared value, and customs supervision area
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical exports, evidencing that the goods left China and naming the foreign consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the equivalent bank foreign-currency receipts evidencing that export consideration has been repatriated to a Chinese bank account
  • For offshore services and digital content licensing: cross-border service or licence agreement identifying the non-resident customer and the exact scope of the supplied digital service
  • For offshore services and digital content licensing: zero-rated e-invoice, bank payment receipt, platform sales record, and any geo-location, IP, or user-location evidence indicating consumption outside mainland China

China operates a fully tax-controlled electronic invoice regime. All VAT invoices, both ordinary and special, must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's approved e-invoice platforms or through tax-control devices connected to the Golden Tax information system. Under the full digital electronic invoice system, invoice creation, transmission, and red-letter reversal are uploaded to the STA cloud platform in real time, and each invoice receives a unique digital invoice number and anti-counterfeit verification code. The system automatically reconciles invoice data with VAT returns, customs export declarations, foreign-currency receipts, and bank settlement data; any mismatch can suspend invoice issuance quotas and trigger automated risk alerts.

The STA and Customs apply post-transaction matching to every zero-rated export and offshore service claim. If the tax authority finds that the zero-rating was claimed without a genuine offshore supply, without required documentation, or without repatriation of foreign exchange within the statutory deadline, the zero-rating is reversed and VAT is assessed at the applicable domestic rate (generally 6% for services or 13% for goods). The taxpayer must then pay the unpaid tax plus late-payment interest. Under the Tax Collection Administration Law, tax fraud and false export tax refund claims carry fines of 50% to five times the tax evaded or underpaid, downgrading of taxpayer credit rating, suspension of export rebate privileges, forced joint enforcement actions, and, for serious cases, criminal liability for false export tax refund fraud. The tax recovery window is generally three years, but for fraudulent or concealing conduct it extends to five years and may lead to additional criminal enforcement beyond the administrative clawback period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese mainland maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime under the authority of the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) acting as the operational regulator for current account and capital account foreign exchange transactions. The renminbi is fully convertible for bona fide current account items, including trade in goods and services such as stock photography licensing and generative visual asset subscriptions, provided that the underlying transaction is genuine, documented, and verified by the designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account items remain heavily regulated: foreign direct investment into a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) requires registration with SAFE, a capital account information system filing, and the opening of a Renminbi Capital Account or Foreign Currency Capital Account at a domestic bank. Surrender requirements mandate that export proceeds in foreign currency be repatriated into China and converted or retained in accordance with SAFE's foreign currency account rules; mainstream practice for low-risk service exports requires settlement within the statutory timeframe once the customs or service trade verification documents are filed. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong SAR parent entity is permitted after annual audit, completion of corporate income tax final settlement, and submission of the Foreign Exchange Payment Order for Profit Remittance, including board resolution, audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and FDI registration credentials. However, operational friction remains in the form of bank risk-based due diligence on the beneficial ownership chain, verification of the commercial substance of digital asset marketplace revenues, and potential delays when the declared revenue pattern involves high-frequency small-ticket cross-border e-commerce receipts. The WFOE must maintain a SAFE-registered foreign debt or capital injection record, and any significant movement of funds out of the scope of registered business requires case-by-case approval. In practice, the Hong Kong holding entity should fund the WFOE through a properly documented equity capital injection, maintain a clean audit trail of intellectual property licensing royalties, and route trade collections through domestic collection accounts to minimize convertibility lag. Overall, currency convertibility is achievable for the stated low-risk commercial stock photo AI business, but only with strict adherence to SAFE filing windows, bank-prescribed purpose codes, and documentary evidence for each cross-border flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into China within the statutory time limit, generally 180 days from the date of export or service completion, and deposited into a domestic foreign currency settlement account at a designated bank. Under current SAFE practice, the mandatory conversion ratio for current account receipts is not a fixed percentage; the WFOE may retain foreign currency balances in its settlement account within approved limits, but any conversion to renminbi must be supported by the relevant service trade contract, invoice, and the Foreign Exchange Receipt and Payment Confirmation. For service export revenue such as subscription fees and royalty income from stock photo licensing, the bank will verify the authenticity of the transaction through the Service Trade Foreign Exchange Administration system, and proceeds must be surrendered or converted within the documented settlement timeline, typically no later than the bank's required processing window after the receipt is credited.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Currenxie have limited or no legal viability as the primary banking rail for the China mainland WFOE. They are not licensed as domestic deposit-taking institutions by the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission or the PBOC, and they cannot provide access to the China Mainland's CIPS or SAFE-regulated capital account system. They may be used only as auxiliary treasury tools for collecting low-frequency overseas sales from non-China customers, but the WFOE must mandatorily maintain a domestic bank account for statutory capital injection, tax payment, payroll, and profit repatriation. The use of unlicensed EMI rails for the core operating account would create a KYC gap, violate SAFE's requirement that designated foreign exchange banks verify cross-border flows, and expose the entity to regulatory penalties for illegal fund settlement. Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury operations, Medium only for ancillary receivable collection in foreign jurisdictions outside China.

The Hong Kong parent must remit equity capital into the WFOE's SAFE-registered Capital Account at the designated domestic bank using SWIFT MT103 with a clear remittance purpose code such as 'FDI Equity Subscription' or the appropriate SAFE capital project code. The remittance instruction must reference the WFOE's FDI registration certificate, the capital account registration number, and the business license. The domestic receiving bank must carry out a capital account information system verification, and the funds must be held in an escrowed capital account until a capital verification report is issued by a licensed CPA firm. No disbursement from the capital account is permitted before the capital verification report and SAFE registration are completed. Current account inflows from overseas sales, such as stock photo licensing fees, must be routed through the operational foreign currency settlement account with proper service trade purpose codes and supporting contracts. All fund routing must be executed only through domestic designated foreign exchange banks; any attempt to bypass the domestic banking system through fintech EMIs or wallet-based clearing would constitute illegal cross-border settlement.For outbound trade settlements, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for direct remittances, Commercial Letters of Credit for higher-value structured transactions, and Documentary Collections only for low-risk, well-documented trade flows. Since the business is a digital stock photography marketplace, the primary clearing rail will be MT103 direct wire transfers to overseas photographers, AI infrastructure vendors, cloud service providers, and the Hong Kong parent for service fees. Each outgoing payment must be supported by an invoice, contract, withholding tax certificate if applicable, and a foreign exchange payment form filed with the domestic bank under the relevant service trade purpose code. The bank will review the transaction against the SAFE service trade data system and may request additional proof of performance, such as delivery logs, usage-based royalty reports, or API consumption records from AI stock photo platforms. For intellectual property royalties paid to foreign licensors, the WFOE must apply withholding tax registration and submit the relevant tax withholding certificate to the bank to facilitate the remittance.Onshore RMB/USD and RMB/HKD deliverable forward contracts at domestic designated banks, subject to SAFE's real demand principle and underlying exposure validation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only for offshore entities in the Hong Kong market, not available to the onshore WFOE for hedging its RMB exposure.Cross-currency swaps permitted only where the WFOE has a certified underlying foreign currency debt or investment exposure and obtains SAFE approval for a genuine hedging transaction.FX options embedded in structured deposits or plain vanilla foreign exchange forwards are permissible if strictly hedging current account or registered capital exposures.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMIs such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Currenxie have limited or no legal viability as the primary banking rail for the China mainland WFOE. They are not licensed as domestic deposit-taking institutions by the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission or the PBOC, and they cannot provide access to the China Mainland's CIPS or SAFE-regulated capital account system. They may be used only as auxiliary treasury tools for collecting low-frequency overseas sales from non-China customers, but the WFOE must mandatorily maintain a domestic bank account for statutory capital injection, tax payment, payroll, and profit repatriation. The use of unlicensed EMI rails for the core operating account would create a KYC gap, violate SAFE's requirement that designated foreign exchange banks verify cross-border flows, and expose the entity to regulatory penalties for illegal fund settlement. Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury operations, Medium only for ancillary receivable collection in foreign jurisdictions outside China.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice [2016] No.42, contemporaneous Local File documentation is required where the WFOE's annual related-party tangible-property transfers exceed RMB 200 million, intangible-asset or financial-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or related-party service transactions exceed RMB 40 million; a Master File is required where total related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion or where the SAT specifically requests it. For cross-border transactions below those levels, the Annual Related-Party Transaction Reporting schedules generally require disclosure once related-party transactions exceed the applicable de minimis levels.

Economic substance scope: The PRC WFOE must maintain real economic substance in China, including personnel, premises, contracts, servers and management functions sufficient to carry out core income-generating activities (CIGA); transfer-pricing audits may recharacterize merely contractual arrangements. If the Hong Kong parent is used to offshore profits, the Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations require the Hong Kong entity to conduct CIGA in Hong Kong, such as board meetings, strategic policy decisions, substantive staff, and active IP management; failure to do so will cause both the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department and the PRC SAT to deny treaty benefits, apply GAAR, and impose 10% withholding on distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company Arrangement and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: PRC GAAR under Article 47 of the Corporate Income Tax Law and Article 120 of the Implementation Regulations allows the tax authority to recharacterize the arrangement, disregard the Hong Kong shareholder and impose the full 10% dividend withholding plus 0.05% daily surcharge; withholding agents that fail to withhold are fined 50%-300% of the unwithheld amount under Article 69 of the Tax Administration Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false beneficial-ownership certificates and directed the dividend diversion can be jointly and severally liable to restitution of the withheld tax from personal assets; the tax authority may issue an exit-control order (travel ban) against them; where the amount is large and constitutes intentional tax evasion, criminal liability under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law exposes them to imprisonment from three to seven years and confiscation of the diverted assets.
Transfer Pricing Fraud and False Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under Article 41 of the CIT Law and Public Notice [2016] No.42, the SAT may make an arm's-length re-determination, add back the excessive fees and understated revenue, impose 0.05% daily interest on the deficiency, and levy a penalty from 50% to five times the underpaid tax under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law; false documentation can be separately treated as tax evasion under Article 204 of the Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who deliberately created false local-file documentation may be subjected to civil asset recovery of the tax deficiency from personal property; the court can impose a restriction order freezing their bank accounts and a travel ban/blacklisting preventing them from leaving China; criminal courts have imposed sentences of up to seven years' imprisonment for tax evasion through fraudulent transfer-pricing documentation, as well as criminal fines.
Thin Capitalization, Hidden Equity and WHT Evasion

Statutory sanction: Article 46 of the CIT Law and Article 119 of the Implementation Regulations disallow the excessive interest and recharacterize the excess as a deemed dividend; the disallowed interest is not deductible and the 10% withholding tax is imposed on the deemed distribution, with a treaty rate of 7% unavailable because the arrangement is not genuine; late withholding and evasion penalties apply under Articles 63 and 69 of the Tax Administration Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved artificially thin-capitalized structures and illicit offshore interest payments are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and related surcharges to the extent they participated in the misdeclaration; the penalty can include blacklisting and travel-restriction orders by the tax authority; intentional evasion of withholding tax exceeding RMB 50,000 may result in criminal imprisonment for the responsible director under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law, with civil restitution of diverted funds to the tax authority.
