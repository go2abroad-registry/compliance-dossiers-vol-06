Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or blocked capital account is required for a Hong Kong private company limited by shares. The intended share capital may be nominal (e.g., HKD 1 per share) and shares may be issued nil-paid, partly-paid, or fully-paid under the Articles of Association. If the Hong Kong vehicle later subscribes into a PRC WFOE, the PRC entity's registered capital must be contributed by the deadline in its articles and foreign exchange registration with SAFE will apply; these PRC requirements are separate from the Hong Kong formation and do not impose any escrow condition on the Hong Kong company.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing through the China Electronic Taxpayer portal (e-Tax @ the official website of the State Taxation Administration).

E-government filing portal

For a China WFOE, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation during company incorporation serves as the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all tax purposes. The foreign parent does not need a separate application; within 30 days after business license issuance, the WFOE must complete tax registration and first reporting through the electronic tax office. For foreign non-resident enterprises without a permanent establishment, a temporary taxpayer identification or withholding registration is required through the competent tax authority for any China-source income, using the dedicated non-resident tax registration module.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated for VAT, provided the goods physically leave China and customs export procedures are completed. For cross-border services — including professional translation and media localization services — zero-rating is available only where the service is consumed entirely outside China and the recipient is a foreign entity; qualifying services are zero-rated, while others may be exempt. The taxpayer must retain export contracts, overseas payment proof, and foreign-exchange settlement records to evidence the foreign consumption.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (出口货物报关单) with customs release stamp
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export shipping
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Income Receipt Voucher (涉外收入申报单) / Foreign Exchange Settlement Slip
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice
  • Zero-rated VAT export sales declaration (as filed in the VAT return)

China mandates the fully digitalized electronic invoice (digital e-invoice) under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. All VAT invoices must be issued and transmitted through the State Taxation Administration's electronic invoice service platform in real time, with taxpayer digital signatures and fiscal verification through the tax authority's central systems. Conventional paper invoices and local e-invoices are being replaced; batch upload and API integration are required for all general taxpayers.

Export VAT refunds are subject to rigorous tax-customs-banking data triangulation. Any false declarations, missing foreign-exchange repatriation, or forged export documents trigger clawback of refunded VAT plus late-payment surcharge, administrative penalties of up to 50% of the fraudulently obtained refund, and criminal liability for tax fraud. The tax authority can deny refunds and impose a three-year audit exposure period, with unlimited clawback for fraudulent conduct.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with current-account convertibility for genuine trade and service transactions, but capital-account governance remains registration-based and transaction-by-transaction. A mainland China WFOE receiving equity from its Hong Kong parent must complete Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration through the local SAFE branch or a designated bank before any inbound remittance can be credited. The injected foreign currency is held in a capital account and can only be converted into RMB after the bank verifies the underlying capital project purpose against the valid business scope; proceeds may not be diverted to prohibited sectors such as equity investment or real estate unless explicitly approved. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is a current-account outflow, but the WFOE must first pass an annual audit, distribute earnings according to the articles, pay enterprise income tax, obtain the prescribed tax payment certificate for service/trade income remittance, and provide a board resolution declaring the dividend. Bonded or free-trade-account structures can ease cash sweeping, yet they do not remove SAFE's foreign debt or direct-investment limits. Export receipts from localization services are subject to the Trade in Goods/Services authenticity verification system; punitive measures apply where documentary evidence is incomplete or where the timing of intercompany invoices does not match the actual delivery milestones. Overall, the WFOE's treasury must treat RMB and foreign-currency accounts as two carefully reconciled pools, with every inward and outward line item mapped to a registrable SAFE purpose code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE does not impose a mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts; exporters may lawfully retain approved proceeds in a domestic current-account foreign exchange account or voluntarily convert them into RMB at the market rate. For goods export, funds must generally be repatriated into China and collected within 90 days of the export customs declaration unless the company has obtained a legitimate offshore retention arrangement. For service export revenues generated by multilingual localization, translation and dubbing, the statutory requirement is authenticity-based verification: signed contracts, detailed invoices, proof of performance and tax filings must evidence that the cross-border receipt is genuine. In practice, the WFOE should route every export receipt through a designated SAFE-reporting bank, post received funds to the foreign exchange current account, and ensure the service delivery record and PRC tax position are consistent before any conversion or expenditure is approved.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low - foreign fintech EMIs such as Wise B2B and Payoneer are legally barred from acting as the primary banking and clearing infrastructure for a PRC WFOE. Neither holds a domestic banking license under PBOC or the National Financial Regulatory Administration, cannot open RMB current accounts for the entity, cannot fulfill SAFE FDI registration and FX purpose-code reporting, and cannot process statutory tax-certificate-backed dividend outflows. They may be used only as ancillary collection wallets for small overseas localization payments, and even that use must be followed by formal settlement into a tier-1 domestic bank with the underlying transaction reported through the bank's SAFE interface. Relying on such EMIs for capital injection, export proceeds repatriation or corporate liquidity would create irremediable AML and regulatory gaps.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% of the gross dividend under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law for a non-resident enterprise receiving China-source dividends, before any tax treaty relief; reduced to 5% under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement where the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident company directly holding at least 25% of the WFOE's registered capital, and 10% in other cases.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return and final CIT payment must be lodged within 5 months after the calendar fiscal year-end (31 May of the following year for a 31 December year-end). Interim quarterly CIT prepayments are due within 15 days after the end of each quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed any of: RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers; RMB 50 million for intangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; or RMB 40 million for related-party services. Master File is required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue reaches RMB 5 billion. In addition, the WFOE must file the Annual Related Party Transactions Reporting Forms (G series) together with the CIT annual return whenever related-party transactions exist, irrespective of turnover.

Economic substance scope: The PRC general anti-avoidance rule, substance-over-form doctrine, and treaty LOB and beneficial-ownership rules require the WFOE to have real personnel, physical premises, business records, and core income-generating activities in China. Where the HK parent holds the WFOE, the HK company must separately satisfy Hong Kong's Economic Substance Requirements applicable to a pure equity-holding entity (registered office, sufficient corporate governance, records, and local resident officer), particularly when relying on the 5% dividend rate. Artificial arrangements with no commercial rationality are recharacterized and denied treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Books and Records

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law: recovery of underpaid tax, daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge, and a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Criminal Law Article 201: imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines for directors and other responsible persons in serious tax-evasion cases, with the entity also fined.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law where directors or shareholders abuse limited liability to evade tax; directors can be jointly and severally liable for restitution of the company's unpaid tax and civil damages. Major tax violation blacklisting imposes credit downgrades and travel or exit bans, and criminal imprisonment applies to personally culpable directors.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard the arrangement, recharacterize transactions, adjust taxable income by upward transfer-pricing assessment, impose compensatory interest equal to the benchmark RMB loan rate plus 5 percentage points on the underpaid CIT, and levy administrative fines for failure to provide contemporaneous documentation or related-party information. The retroactive adjustment period may extend up to 10 years in abusive cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved artificial pricing and false documentation may be treated as responsible persons in an entity tax crime, risking personal fines, asset seizure for restitution, tax blacklisting, and travel bans; civil liability for causing the company to evade tax may be asserted by the tax authority in enforcement proceedings.
Shell Company and Treaty/DTA Abuse (Beneficial Ownership Failure)

Statutory sanction: Article 47 of the PRC EIT Law and the general anti-avoidance legislation allow the tax authority to recharacterize the conduit and deny treaty benefits; the difference between the 5% treaty rate and 10% statutory WHT, plus default interest and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid WHT, is assessed. Fraudulent treaty-benefit filings also result in administrative fines and publication as a serious tax violation.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE and the HK parent may be held personally liable for the tax shortfall under shadow-directorship and alter-ego piercing, in addition to criminal exposure for fraud under Criminal Law Article 201 if false statements are used. Individual responsible persons face exit bans, blacklisting, asset restitution orders, and imprisonment for up to 7 years.
