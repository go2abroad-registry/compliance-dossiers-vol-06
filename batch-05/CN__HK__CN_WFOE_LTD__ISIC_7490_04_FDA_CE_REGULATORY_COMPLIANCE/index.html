Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: the STA-coordinated Electronic Tax Bureau system, accessed through the taxpayer's provincial-level electronic tax bureau portal.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), which is automatically recognized by the tax administration as the taxpayer identification number (TIN) under the 'one license with one code' multi-registration reform. No separate TIN application is normally required. The WFOE must, within 30 days after obtaining the business license, complete supplementary tax registration and taxpayer-qualification information through the Electronic Tax Bureau, including legal representative identification, articles of association, capital structure, banking account details, expected scope of taxable services, and VAT taxpayer status election between General VAT Taxpayer and Small-Scale VAT Taxpayer. A non-resident Hong Kong parent that does not carry on business through a permanent establishment in China is not normally required to obtain a Chinese TIN; it is identified through the non-resident enterprise contract filing and withholding registration procedure with the competent tax authority.

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales for mandatory registration as a General VAT Taxpayer; all businesses carrying on taxable activities are required to complete tax registration regardless of turnover, and businesses below this threshold may register as Small-Scale VAT Taxpayers or elect General VAT Taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for VAT zero-rating or export rebate when the goods are exported from the customs territory of China and the export proceeds are received in accordance with SAFE foreign exchange rules. For services, the regulatory consulting services in the FDA 510(k) and CE marking scope are treated as cross-border services; such services are eligible for VAT exemption, and in limited cases zero-rating, when supplied to an overseas entity, the service is actually consumed wholly outside China, no VAT is passed on under the contract, and the consideration is received from outside China. If the service includes a zero-rated offshore digital service component, such as software or data processing, that component must be separately contracted and supported by the zero-rated offshore services evidence file.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (electronic customs clearance record issued through the China International Trade Single Window) evidencing physical exportation from a Chinese customs port
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List matching the customs declaration in quantity, value, packing, destination, and Incoterms
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, or Air Waybill issued by a licensed carrier showing China as the port of loading and the foreign destination as the port of discharge
  • Export sales contract or purchase order evidencing the overseas buyer, product description, and contractual terms of sale
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt record or SAFE trade-in-foreign-exchange monitoring data evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the overseas buyer in the WFOE's designated bank account
  • Export tax rebate declaration submitted through the Electronic Tax Bureau together with any destination-country certification or third-country transit documentation required by customs
  • Zero-rated or exempt cross-border service substantiation file for consulting revenue, comprising service request, service delivery evidence, overseas consumption statement, contract, and foreign-exchange receipt record

China operates the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice system. Taxpayers issue digital invoices through the Electronic Invoice Service Platform, which transmits invoice data in real time to the Golden Tax Phase IV anti-fraud system. Each digital invoice carries a unique invoice code, a digital signature, and an encrypted anti-tampering stamp managed through the taxpayer's Tax Digital Account. Invoice quotas and zero-rated or exempt cross-border transaction documentation must be reconciled through the Electronic Tax Bureau before the invoice is cleared for export zero-rating or exemption.

The STA may cross-verify zero-rating and exemption claims against customs export declarations, logistics data, digital invoices, bank collection records, and SAFE foreign-exchange monitoring data. False or unsubstantiated claims are treated as tax evasion under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and the criminal provisions against fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds. The tax authority will refuse the refund, claw back any refund or exempted VAT already obtained, impose late-payment surcharges of 0.05% per day, and apply administrative penalties of 50% up to five times the tax amount falsely refunded or evaded. Serious violations may also trigger suspension of export rebate eligibility for up to 36 months and criminal prosecution where the monetary threshold is exceeded.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate system administered by the PBOC and SAFE. The RMB is fully convertible for current account transactions, including payments for trade in goods, services, and remittances of foreign-invested enterprise profits, provided the underlying transaction is bona fide and supported by authentic documentation. Capital account transactions remain strictly controlled, and any cross-border flow classified as capital, such as foreign direct investment contributions, shareholder loans, outward investment, and domestic foreign-currency borrowing, requires prior registration or approval from SAFE or local designated branches. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) operating as a MedTech regulatory consultancy must obtain an FDI-related SAFE registration before receiving equity capital from its Hong Kong parent, and all capital account receipts must be credited to a special capital account (capital escrow account) at a designated domestic bank. Profit remittance to offshore shareholders is permitted only after annual statutory audit, corporate income tax filing, and tax clearance, with the remitting bank verifying a board resolution for distribution and the non-reserve requirements under the PRC Company Law. Currency surrender requirements are practically relevant for exporters, but a consulting WFOE will predominantly earn domestic RMB, thus offshore FX settlement is driven by capital account regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under prevailing SAFE rules, export proceeds must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of customs exportation. There is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio; proceeds can be retained in a foreign currency export account, but any conversion into RMB must be conducted through a designated bank with submission of the export customs declaration, logistics documentation, and commercial invoices. Full surrender is effectively required for the RMB settlement portion related to domestic obligations. Enterprises must monitor their trade counterfeit inspection status and maintain records for the SAFE suspicious trade screening.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated FX regulatory authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable. Mainland China does not permit offshore digital EMI or virtual bank entities to provide RMB or foreign currency accounts to domestic enterprises. Only licensed domestic banks and SAFE-designated banks may open corporate accounts and handle cross-border settlements. Third-party payment institutions (e.g., licensed payment companies) can support collection of domestic consumer or B2B payments only under a bank cooperation model and cannot substitute for regulatory banking rails for treasury management. Any offshore EMI claiming to service a mainland WFOE will be non-compliant and banks will be required to reject or freeze related funds.

Turnover: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) Part 9, a Hong Kong company must be audited by a certified public accountant unless it qualifies for statutory exemption. A private company is audit-exempt for a financial year if it is a small private company and, where relevant, the group conditions are satisfied. The small private company test is met by satisfying at least two of the following three thresholds for the financial year: (1) total revenue not exceeding HK$10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,282,000); (2) total assets not exceeding HK$10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,282,000); (3) not more than 50 employees. Dormant companies may also claim a separate waiver. If any threshold is failed, if the entity is part of a non-qualifying group, or if an audited report is required by another regulator or the Inland Revenue Ordinance, a full independent audit by a Hong Kong CPA in accordance with Hong Kong Standards on Auditing is mandatory.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on outbound dividends paid by the PRC WFOE to a Hong Kong non-resident enterprise, absent treaty relief; the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement may reduce the rate to 5% if the Hong Kong beneficial owner is a company holding at least 25% of the WFOE's paid-up capital at the dividend-payment date and meets substance requirements.
