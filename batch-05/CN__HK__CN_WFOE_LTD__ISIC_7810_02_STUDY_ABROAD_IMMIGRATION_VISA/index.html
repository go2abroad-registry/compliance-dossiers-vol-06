Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic filing is administered through the national Electronic Tax Bureau portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn and export-related filings through the China Customs International Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in mainland China, the entity must first be registered with the local branch of the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) to obtain a Business License bearing a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Within 30 calendar days after the business license is issued, the WFOE must complete taxpayer registration on the STA Electronic Tax Bureau using the USCC; no separate paper TIN certificate is issued, and the USCC is the statutory taxpayer identification number for all tax filings, including VAT and Enterprise Income Tax. The registration profile must include the legal representative, registered address, business scope, accounting standards, banking details, and expected annual turnover, which determines the VAT taxpayer status category. Foreign non-resident shareholders do not obtain a Chinese TIN solely by reason of equity ownership; if a foreign entity has no establishment or business office in China, the Chinese payer performs withholding tax registration on behalf of the non-resident payee.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the WFOE is a China-resident enterprise under Enterprise Income Tax law, taxed on worldwide taxable income at the statutory 25% corporate income tax rate, and is not eligible for pass-through treatment.

Available election forms

None for corporate income tax classification; China does not provide a check-the-box election for foreign-owned entities. For VAT only, a taxpayer may elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer even if annual taxable turnover is below the RMB 5,000,000 mandatory threshold, subject to fixed business premises, independent bookkeeping, and STA approval; otherwise small-scale taxpayer status is the default. No hybrid mismatch elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must register for VAT upon establishment with no monetary threshold; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered once cumulative annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000. The WFOE's study-abroad and immigration visa consulting and advisory services are classified as modern services subject to the 6% VAT rate, while the headline standard VAT rate on goods is 13%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from China where the customs export declaration and outbound customs records confirm the goods left the customs territory. For cross-border services, zero-rating or exemption is available under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and related STA administrative announcements when the purchaser is a non-resident enterprise, the service is provided entirely or consumed wholly outside China, and the consideration is received in foreign exchange. For study-abroad placement and immigration visa advisory services, the service contract, proof of the client's foreign residence, evidence of performance outside China, and bank receipt of foreign currency are the controlling documents. Services supplied to Chinese-resident students for overseas study placement are not export zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration of Goods (export goods declaration filed through the China International Trade Single Window and accepted by China Customs)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing outbound movement of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or banking record of foreign exchange receipt and settlement under SAFE export proceeds verification requirements
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Export sales contract or order evidencing the overseas buyer and CIF/FOB terms

The WFOE must issue all VAT invoices through the STA-administered Full Digital Electronic Invoice (all-electronic e-invoice) system and connected tax-control invoice systems; invoice data is transmitted in real time to the STA, and each e-invoice carries a unique nationwide invoice code, machine-printed code, and QR anti-fraud element. Taxpayers integrate their accounting or ERP systems with the official e-invoice service platform through STA-approved APIs for automated issuance and real-time reporting. There is no private invoicing clearance intermediary; the STA platform performs digital authentication, rejects duplicate invoice codes, and enables recipients to verify any invoice through the national E-invoice Verification Service. Legacy paper invoices, where approved, must still be created through tax-control instruments and reported before issuance.

Export zero-rating and refund claims are scrutinized by the STA and China Customs through post-clearance audit and tax inspection. If a refund is erroneous, the 180-day foreign exchange repatriation condition is not met, or the supporting documentation is unverified, the claimed output VAT exemption or input VAT refund is clawed back with statutory interest from the date of refund. Under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, fraudulent acts resulting in non-payment or underpayment of tax are subject to a penalty of 50% to five times the amount underpaid; criminal liability may arise under the Criminal Law provisions on defrauding export tax refunds where intent is established, including imprisonment and fines. The responsible taxpayer may additionally be placed on the tax dishonesty blacklist, face exit restrictions, and lose future export tax refund facilitation status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is not freely convertible in the capital account. The People's Republic of China operates under a 'convertibility on the current account, registration plus supervision on the capital account' framework administered by SAFE. A WFOE in the study-abroad and immigration advisory sector is a resident enterprise. Its USD, HKD, or EUR income from service exports is a current-account item, and the bank will require authentic contracts, invoices, service-completion records, and tax declarations before converting foreign currency into RMB. Capital-account receipts, especially the paid-in capital injected by the Hong Kong shareholder, must first obtain SAFE FDI registration and be deposited in a dedicated foreign-currency capital account. Once funds are in the capital account, conversion to RMB is permitted, but the resulting RMB deposits are subject to the 'negative list' use commitment: they may be used for salaries, office rental, procurement of domestic services and raw materials, fixed-asset purchases, repayment of RMB loans, and other self-use operating expenses. Profit remittance of after-tax retained earnings to the Hong Kong parent is lawful but requires an annual statutory audit, corporate income tax settlement certificate, foreign-exchange registration, and a board resolution declaring dividends. The bank will also screen for beneficial ownership and enforce anti-offshore-shell requirements. Consequently, the WFOE should maintain strong documentation from the date of inception.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE current-account convertibility system, there is no mandatory export proceeds surrender ratio. The WFOE may retain 100% of its export and cross-border service receipts in an onshore foreign-currency settlement account or voluntarily convert them to RMB. However, the funds must be repatriated to China no later than 180 days after the invoice date or by the contractually agreed settlement date, whichever is earlier, and the bank must verify the receipt through SAFE's trade monitoring and BOP reporting systems. For ISIC 7810 cross-border advisory services, the export declaration is replaced by the signed service agreement, progress certificates, and tax receipts, and any deferral beyond 180 days requires a documented change order and bank filing.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, administered under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not a viable primary banking rail for a PRC WFOE. They are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE as deposit-taking institutions, cannot participate directly in CNAPS/CFXPS RMB clearing, and are legally barred from opening domestic settlement accounts for Chinese resident enterprises. An EMI such as Airwallex, Payoneer, or Wise may be engaged only as an ancillary payment processor if it operates through a fully licensed Chinese commercial bank and holds a PBOC-approved cross-border payment license, but it cannot fulfill the FDI capital registration, capital verification, tax custody, or profit-remittance requirements. Feasibility is Low for treasury account opening and Medium only for niche cross-border collection support where a licensed local bank partner is used.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, a Local File must be contemporaneously prepared when the WFOE's annual related-party transactions exceed any of the following thresholds: RMB 200 million for tangible goods transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 50 million for related-party service/labour transactions. A Master File is required when the foreign parent group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion and the WFOE has related-party transactions, or when the WFOE alone has related-party transactions exceeding RMB 1 billion. Documentation must be submitted within 15 days after the STA's request.

Economic substance scope: Although the Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) technically apply to Hong Kong-resident companies, the PRC tax authorities apply an equivalent economic substance and beneficial ownership scrutiny to treaty claims and transfer pricing adjustments. The WFOE must demonstrate through its bank statements, payroll, leases, client contracts, compliance manuals, and board minutes that the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for study-abroad placement and immigration visa advisory services are performed in mainland China. If the WFOE is a shell that merely receives commission while the Hong Kong parent actually manages the client relationships, the STA may reattribute the income to the Hong Kong entity or deny the CIT deduction for the intercompany fees; conversely, if the Hong Kong parent is a conduit without substance, the treaty dividend, interest, and royalty relief will be denied, resulting in the 10% statutory WHT being collected with interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Base Erosion and Treaty Conduit Arrangements

Statutory sanction: The STA may, under CIT Law Art. 41, Art. 47 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules under Art. 120 of the Implementation Regulations, recharacterise the arrangement, disallow the deduction, recompute taxable income at 25% CIT, impose a daily late surcharge at 0.05%, and levy a penalty of 50% to 100% of the underpaid tax under Art. 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law. Criminal referral under Criminal Law Art. 201 is triggered where the tax evasion exceeds statutory thresholds.

Director personal liability: The directors who approved the sham agreements are jointly and severally liable for the WFOE's unpaid CIT, penalties and surcharges under the corporate veil-piercing doctrine in PRC Company Law Art. 20; the tax authority can apply for property preservation and exit-control (travel bans) against the legal representative and directors; criminal liability includes imprisonment up to 7 years and confiscation of property for tax evasion.
Failure to Withhold Statutory WHT on Outbound Dividends, Interest and Royalties

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 69 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law, the withholding agent (the WFOE) is liable for the unpaid withheld tax and may be penalised at a rate of 50% to 100% of the amount that should have been withheld; the non-resident beneficiary remains subject to the tax, and the STA will additionally collect daily surcharges of 0.05% from the date of withholding default; repeat non-compliance can escalate to administrative closure and revocation of the export/tax registration.

Director personal liability: Directors and the financial controller who knowingly signed erroneous withholding declarations are personally liable to the WFOE for resulting tax, penalties and loss; the STA can blacklist the legal representative and directors, impose travel restrictions, and require restitution of corporate assets; in aggravated cases where the non-withheld funds are misappropriated, criminal liability under Criminal Law Art. 273 for default in payment of tax or Art. 272 for misappropriation of enterprise funds may apply, with imprisonment from 3 to 10 years.
Operating Study-Abroad and Immigration Advisory Services Without Statutory Licensing and AML Compliance

Statutory sanction: The PRC Administrative Licensing Law and the Measures for the Administration of Intermediary Service Institutions for Self-Funded Study Abroad allow the market supervision authority to confiscate illegal income, impose fines up to RMB 100,000, issue suspension orders and revoke the business licence; the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law provides fines up to RMB 5 million and suspension of relevant operations for entities that fail to comply with KYC, customer due diligence and suspicious transaction reporting; where client funds are misappropriated or the consultancy is used as a channel for capital flight, criminal prosecution follows.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law Art. 20 where the WFOE is a mere shell, requiring directors and the actual controller personally to refund client deposits and escrow balances, compensate harmed immigrants, and disgorge unlawful gains; civil courts may issue asset freezes and travel bans; criminal liability under Criminal Law Art. 266 for fraud and Art. 191 for money laundering can result in imprisonment from 3 to 10 years or more, with heavy fines and confiscation of personal property.
