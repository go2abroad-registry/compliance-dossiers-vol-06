Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: unified Electronic Tax Bureau platform (etax.chinatax.gov.cn).

E-government filing portal

For a Chinese WFOE, the Tax Identification Number (TIN) is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) granted at incorporation. Step 1: Complete company incorporation with the local Market Supervision Administration (SAMR) after obtaining foreign investment approval/record-filing, receiving a Business License with USCC. Step 2: Within 30 days of Business License issuance, perform tax registration through the Electronic Tax Bureau by submitting the business license, articles of association, legal representative identification, registered address proof, and bank account details. Step 3: The competent tax authority confirms tax types (VAT, Corporate Income Tax, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, stamp tax, etc.) and activates the taxpayer account. Step 4: The USCC is the statutory TIN; no separate physical tax certificate is issued. Step 5: Apply for invoice issuance quota and register for VAT general taxpayer status if expected annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5 million (or voluntarily). Step 6: Set up electronic invoicing credentials and any required tax software.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The WFOE is a Chinese resident limited liability enterprise, taxed on worldwide income at the standard 25% Corporate Income Tax rate. No pass-through treatment applies. Hong Kong SAR is the offshore parent/invoicing jurisdiction and does not alter the WFOE's Chinese resident tax status.

Available election forms

None - China does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. The only relevant entity-level election is VAT status: small-scale taxpayer (simplified collection at 3% or currently 1%) vs. VAT general taxpayer (input credit available), which is determined by annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5 million or voluntary registration. Certain Corporate Income Tax incentive elections (e.g., High and New Technology Enterprise at 15%) may be available if strict R&D and revenue criteria are satisfied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for WFOE tax registration; tax registration is mandatory within 30 days of incorporation. For VAT classification, annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 trigger mandatory general taxpayer status; below that, small-scale taxpayer treatment may apply, with a current monthly VAT exemption of RMB 100,000 for small-scale taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% VAT applies if a valid customs export declaration is filed and the goods physically leave Chinese customs territory. For services, including qualifying remote BPO and call-center services, 0% VAT applies when the service is rendered to a non-resident enterprise, the service contract expressly provides for offshore consumption, the payment is received in foreign currency from outside China, and the service does not involve domestic onshore delivery or local consumption. The WFOE must record the zero-rated export service in the Electronic Tax Bureau and retain all documentary evidence for audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) - for goods exports only
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill - for goods exports only
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / bank foreign-currency receipt voucher - required for both goods and services export proceeds
  • For services only: signed cross-border service contract, commercial invoice, evidence of overseas consumption (e.g., call logs, service-level reporting), and export transaction list filed in the e-tax system

China mandates all VAT invoices be issued via the STA-controlled electronic invoice system under the Golden Tax IV architecture. Fully digital electronic invoices are generated on the Electronic Tax Bureau platform or through certified API integrations, with real-time transmission of invoice data to the STA database. Every invoice carries a unique digital serial number, an anti-fraud QR code, and a digital signature. Physical tax-control devices are no longer required for fully digital invoices. Invoice data is automatically reconciled against VAT returns, and any discrepancy or out-of-pattern issuance triggers automatic tax authority alerts.

The STA and Customs jointly scrutinize export zero-rating claims. If export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days without an approved extension, the claimed 0% VAT is retroactively clawed back as domestic taxable sales, with late-payment interest. Under Article 66 of the Law on Administration of Tax Collection and Article 204 of the Criminal Law, fraudulent export refunds or fabricated documentation result in repayment of the refund, a penalty of 0.5 to 5 times the tax involved, confiscation of illegal gains, and potential criminal liability. Evidence of sham contracts, fake customs declarations, or inflated service invoices will trigger a full audit, disallowance of the refund, and listing on the tax-risk supervision file.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a current-account convertibility regime under IMF Article VIII, while capital-account transactions remain broadly controlled. The RMB is fully convertible for trade, services, dividends, and wages subject to real-transaction documentation; SAFE verifies authenticity. Capital account items such as FDI equity injection, cross-border loans, and external guarantees require separate registration, approval, or negative-list compliance. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in BPO call center services, operating expenses in RMB are settled locally; foreign currency receipts from offshore clients must be repatriated into China unless retained in a domestic FX account under SAFE rules. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong parent require audited financial statements, tax clearance (Corporate Income Tax settlement), and a board resolution; remittance is processed through the current account as dividend distribution. However, the use of registered capital must comply with the Foreign Investment Negative List and the 2015 Foreign Exchange Reform No. 19, which allows voluntary FX settlement but prohibits deployment into real estate or equity investment beyond the approved scope. Surrender of export proceeds is no longer mandatory per circular 2012; but repatriation within the customs declaration verification period is compulsory. Overall, a WFOE can achieve efficient treasury liquidity only through a compliant domestic banking partner, with offshore EMIs legally barred from direct onshore servicing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular 2012 (Jiefa No. 10) and the current Policy on the Administration of Foreign Exchange Accounts under Current Account, export proceeds are subject to the 'Repatriation but no mandatory surrender' rule. All export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated to China within 90 days from the date of customs declaration (for general trade) and deposited in a current-account foreign exchange account. There is no mandatory statutory conversion ratio; an entity may retain the foreign currency until the annual SAFE verification. However, retained balances are subject to the current-account income verification ceiling and must not exceed the 100% upper limit of the past twelve months' total current account receipts. Any excess must be converted into RMB at a bank spot rate.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Wise B2B, Airwallex, or other offshore EMIs are not licensed as banks in mainland China. PBOC and SAFE mandate that all onshore FX settlements must be processed by banks designated by SAFE. Offshore digital EMIs cannot provide RMB clearing, cannot execute an entity-level FX purpose code, and cannot accept capital account injections for a Chinese WFOE. They can only facilitate international wire transfers if the WFOE uses them to remit funds from Hong Kong parent to a domestic bank? Actually inbound capital must come via domestic bank. For outbound payments, the WFOE must use domestic banks. Thus, fintech EMI viability is Low and legally restricted.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent injects registered capital (in USD or HKD) via SWIFT MT103 or CHIPS to the WFOE's capital settlement account at a domestic bank designated by SAFE. The bank will perform a fidelity check against the FDI foreign exchange registration code (FDI number) issued by SAFE and the business license. The remittance must specify the purpose code for 'FDI Equity Injection' exactly as recorded in the FDIControl system. The bank will credit the capital account and apply the 'voluntary conversion' policy allowing the WFOE to convert the foreign currency to RMB at a spot rate, provided the use of RMB complies with the Negative List. A Chinese certified public accountant must subsequently issue a capital verification report to confirm the injection, which is then lodged with SAFE and the local Administration for Market Regulation.For domestic operating expenses, the WFOE will use local RMB payments through CNAPS or the clearing house system. For cross-border vendor payments, the permitted rails include SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) and MT202 (bank-to-bank cover), with the correct SAFE purpose code (e.g., 121 for trade-related charges, 228 for professional services, or 322 for telecommunications/computing services) applied. Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance) may be used for goods-related supply chains, while Documentary Collections are acceptable for low-value recurring service fees. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File (本地文档) mandatory when the aggregate annual amount of related-party transactions (including services such as BPO, call-center support, shared services) exceeds RMB 40 million; or when tangible property transfers exceed RMB 200 million; or intangible property / financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million. A Master File is required when the multinational group's annual related-party transaction turnover in China exceeds RMB 10 billion. All related-party transactions must be covered by a formal Chinese-language TP policy.

Economic substance scope: China does not impose a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation (ESR); however, the PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under CIT Law Art. 47 and Tax Collection Administration Law Art. 36 allows the tax authorities to disregard structures lacking commercial substance. For a Hong Kong shareholder claiming DTA relief, substance is mandatory: the Hong Kong party must demonstrate actual business premises, a full-time board of directors, employees with real payroll, and substantive decision-making in Hong Kong. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) of the WFOE must be performed in China; outsourcing the actual call-center operations to China while booking profits in Hong Kong is prohibited unless economically justified and priced at arm's length.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through False Cost Declarations or Inflated Intercompany Recharges

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law (TCA) Arts. 63-64: civil penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax, plus daily late-payment interest at 0.05%. Criminal Law Art. 201: if the tax evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the payable tax, imprisonment for up to 3 years; if the amount is huge (ordinary >15% and >RMB 500,000), imprisonment for 3-7 years with fines.

Director personal liability: Under the company law and criminal law, the relevant director who expressly ordered or acquiesced to the falsification will be personally liable for restitution of the underpaid tax plus interest. The Chinese tax authority may apply to the court for a travel ban (blacklisting) that prevents the director from leaving mainland China; assets may be frozen. Upon criminal conviction, the director faces imprisonment and confiscation of personal assets used to perpetuate the fraud. The corporate veil is pierced to hold the natural persons accountable, and any Hong Kong-level nominee director is treated as a co-conspirator.
Failure to Maintain Transfer Pricing Documentation and File Related-Party Transactions

Statutory sanction: TCA Art. 62: repeated failure to file relevant schedules results in a fine of RMB 10,000 to RMB 100,000. Under PRC Special Tax Adjustment regulations (Circular 42/2016 and Circular 6/2016), the tax authority may perform an own-adjustment and assess additional CIT at 25%, plus an interest penalty of the one-year PBOC benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points (i.e., effective penalty rate over 8%). If the taxpayer has provided due diligence and documentation reasonably, the interest is reduced. Chronic non-compliance may be escalated to criminal tax evasion under Art. 201 if the underpayment is material and willful.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to obtain the required TP documentation or who sign false transfer-pricing declarations may be held personally liable for the resulting tax deficiency where the corporate form is used as a shell to evade the TP rules. Chinese tax authorities can apply for civil freezing of personal bank accounts and property of the directors. International blacklisting under the PRC 'blacklist for serious tax violations' (CRS information exchange) restricts the director from holding senior officer positions in any Chinese enterprise and may result in a travel ban. In egregious cases, directors face criminal liability for tax evasion as accomplices with imprisonment up to 7 years.
Abusive Treaty Shopping / Use of Hong Kong Mailbox Company to Obtain DTA Relief

Statutory sanction: Denial of the treaty benefit under STA Public Notice No. 9 (2018): the reduced 5% WHT is reversed and the full 10% statutory rate is collected. Under TCA Art. 63, the shortfall is treated as tax evasion; the WFOE as withholding agent is liable for the difference plus interest (0.05% daily) and penalties of 50% to 5 times the shortfall. If the shell structure also involves disguised investment, PRC GAAR (CIT Art. 47) allows the tax authority to reconsider the entire structure, possibly taxing the ultimate parent as a Chinese tax resident, resulting in full 25% CIT on deemed direct profits.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Hong Kong shell and of the Chinese WFOE who signed the beneficial-ownership declarations knowing the shell to be a conduit may be personally liable for the recovered taxes. Chinese courts may pierce the corporate veil where the parent and the Hong Kong shell are wholly controlled and the shell’s separate personality is a sham used to evade tax. Directors face asset restitution, civil injunctions, and travel restrictions. Under PRC Criminal Law Art. 201, the evasion in withholding taxes is a criminal offense carrying imprisonment of up to 7 years; additionally, the directors may be included in the OECD's misleading tax-practices watchlist leading to cross-border exchange-of-information inquiries.
