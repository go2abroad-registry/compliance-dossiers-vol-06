Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA). Online electronic portal: National Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax Bureau) of the State Taxation Administration, accessible through provincial-level E-Tax Bureau portals, the 12366 Taxpayer Service Platform, and Golden Tax Phase IV infrastructure for registration, filing, invoice management, and export tax-refund administration.

E-government filing portal

For the China Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE): (1) Complete formation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the Business License contains the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). (2) Under PRC law, the USCC is the sole statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) for Enterprise Income Tax, VAT, and all other PRC taxes; no separate VAT identification number is issued. (3) SAMR automatically transmits registration data to the State Taxation Administration, which opens the taxpayer file; the WFOE must then complete tax registration particulars in the E-Tax Bureau (legal representative, accounting method, industry classification, bank deposit account, shareholder details) before the first filing period. (4) For cross-border service export involving a zero-rating claim, the WFOE must preserve offshore service contracts and complete any required tax-exemption or zero-rating record-filing with the in-charge tax authority before issuing a zero-rated electronic invoice. (5) For a foreign non-resident entity, including the Hong Kong parent, without a permanent establishment in China, no PRC VAT registration number is ordinarily issued; where such a non-resident makes taxable supplies in China, the domestic recipient is the VAT withholding agent. (6) If a non-resident entity performs a physical project or maintains a taxable establishment in China, it must complete an Organization Temporary Registration with the in-charge tax authority and will be assigned a temporary 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code/TIN for the duration of the activity. All steps are concluded through the E-Tax Bureau portal; no physical visit is generally required except biometric verification of the legal representative or person in charge for high-risk cases.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory when cumulative taxable turnover over any rolling 12-month period exceeds RMB 5,000,000. A taxpayer below that threshold is treated as a small-scale VAT taxpayer; under the current temporary policy through December 31, 2027, small-scale taxpayers with monthly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 are exempt from VAT. Voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer is permitted if the taxpayer has a sound accounting system and satisfies the conditions set by the State Taxation Administration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China legally subscribes to current-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) may freely receive and pay for services and trade on current account, but every transaction must clear SAFE's transaction-by-transaction verification system and the submitting bank's internal control. The Hong Kong parent's capital injection (registered capital) is treated as a capital-account inflow and must be routed into a designated foreign exchange capital account opened at a SAFE-jurisdiction bank. Such funds cannot be used unless the bank has performed foreign exchange registration and the enterprise has provided a genuine and reasonable use plan. Current-account conversion into RMB is allowed without a specific quantitative cap; banks rely on the beneficial informational and documentation basis. Profit repatriation for the WFOE is subject to three sequential hurdles: (i) completion of the annual statutory audit by a PRC CPA firm, (ii) final settlement of corporate income tax and any withholding taxes on dividends with the tax authority, and (iii) formal determination and payment of the dividend under the Foreign Investment Law and SAFE's Foreign Exchange Registration for Direct Investment. There is no mandatory surrender ratio for current-account receipts, but all foreign exchange collected from export or service proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign currency settlement account and be reported under the cross-border payment monitoring system. Offshoreholding and trade structures cannot be used to circumvent these controls without facing administrative penalties and mandatory bank-account restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For current-account merchandise and service receipts, the WFOE must repatriate the foreign exchange proceeds into its domestic foreign currency settlement account within 90 days from the customs declaration date (goods) or the invoice date (services), as monitored by SAFE's trade and payment verification database. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% — there is no compulsory surrender into RMB; instead, the WFOE may elect to keep the proceeds in foreign currency, convert them to RMB, or use them to pay current-account obligations, provided every usage is verified against the original transaction documentation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Low as primary treasury rail, but possible as a supplementary payment actuator. Under current NFRA and SAFE rules, offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and Revolut are not licensed to hold deposit accounts for mainland Chinese legal entities, cannot settle CNY through CIPS, and cannot provide the required foreign exchange registration and verification. They are legally barred from opening a principal bank account for a WFOE. The WFOE may only instruct a licensed domestic bank to execute cross-border wires; if the bank maintains no direct SWIFT relation with the EMI, the transaction will be routed through a correspondent. Accordingly, the WFOE's operational treasury must be domiciled in a Chinese Tier-1 commercial bank, and Wise/B2B rails may only support secondary outbound margin payments where the beneficiary holds an account at the EMI and the underlying contract is fully documented.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent injects registered capital via SWIFT MT103 wire to the WFOE's foreign exchange capital settlement account at a SAFE-designated Tier-1 bank. The payment instruction must include the transaction purpose code '622 - Direct investment in mainland China' (or the current SAFE trade code), the reference 'Registered Capital Injection under the Foreign Investment Law', and the SAFE foreign exchange registration number of the WFOE. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the inbound vesting against the Foreign Investment Negative List and requires the WFOE to present the capital-account opening notice, incorporation certificate, and the lease/purchase contract for the registered office. A PRC CPA must subsequently issue a capital verification report within 30 days of injection; any later conversion of the foreign currency into RMB must be made only for genuine operating expenses, with supporting invoices, and must be reported to SAFE.For services provided to overseas clients, outbound payments from the WFOE include subcontractor fees, licensing fees, and IT infrastructure costs. The primary clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 wire from the WFOE's domestic foreign currency settlement account, supported by a copy of the service contract, the original invoice, proof of service completion, and the withholding tax certificate if the recipient is a PRC tax resident subject to the tax treaty. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are unsuitable for executive-search services because they require transport documents or a bill of lading; they may be used only if the WFOE expands into tangible recruitment merchandising (e.g., test kits, executive assessment products). Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A Hong Kong private limited company is required to have its financial statements audited by an independent Certified Public Accountant (CPA) registered under the Accounting and Financial Reporting Council unless it qualifies for the audit exemption under the Companies Ordinance (Cap 622). The exemption applies to a 'small private company' (or a member of a 'small private company group') satisfying at least two of the following three quantitative criteria for the financial year: (i) total annual revenue does not exceed HK$10,000,000 (USD 1,282,051); (ii) total assets at the end of the financial year do not exceed HK$10,000,000 (USD 1,282,051); and (iii) the average number of employees during the year does not exceed 100. Once a company exceeds two of the thresholds (e.g., revenue over HK$10 million and assets over HK$10 million), a mandatory CPA audit is triggered. A statutory audit is also mandatory for companies that are members of a group unless the group qualifies as a small private company group. Audit exemption is never available for banks, insurance companies, securities dealers, licensed leveraged foreign exchange traders, or any company whose constitutional documents require an audit.
