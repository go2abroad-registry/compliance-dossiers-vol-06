Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online filing and registration through the STA's Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal operated by the competent local tax authority for the WFOE's registered address.

E-government filing portal

After incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the WFOE automatically receives a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the enterprise Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT identification number. Within 30 days from the business license date, the legal representative must complete tax registration in the Electronic Tax Bureau by submitting the business license, articles of association, registered address proof, bank account confirmation, legal representative identification and business scope. The WFOE must then activate VAT status as either a small-scale taxpayer or a general VAT taxpayer; general VAT taxpayer registration is compulsory after annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000. For export activity, the WFOE must complete customs registration through the China International Trade Single Window and register its export tax refund/zero-rating profile in the STA export tax system. The foreign non-resident equity holder (including a Hong Kong SAR parent) does not obtain a separate mainland TIN for passive investment; its China-source taxes are withheld and remitted by the WFOE. Where the foreign company itself creates a PRC permanent establishment or is directly required to issue mainland VAT invoices, it must complete non-resident tax registration in the e-Tax system and obtain a non-resident taxpayer code.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates under a managed floating exchange rate regime with current account convertibility for trade and service transactions, while capital account transactions remain strictly controlled through registration, filing, and designated-bank validation. For a mainland Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) receiving equity capital from a Hong Kong parent, the inflow is classified as foreign direct investment and must comply with SAFE foreign investment information reporting. The funds enter through an onshore foreign exchange capital account and may only be converted into RMB under the capital account settlement mechanism after the bank verifies the investment purpose and business scope. Profit and dividend remittances to the Hong Kong shareholder are treated as current account outflows and are permissible after the WFOE completes audited financial statements, corporate income tax filing, dividend tax withholding, and a bank authenticity review; however, the bank will require the statutory foreign exchange declaration for profit distribution. The RMB remains only partially convertible across the capital account, and proceeds cannot be moved across the border through unlicensed fintech channels. Surrender requirements no longer impose a fixed mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts, but all current account foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated to the onshore banking system and either retained in a foreign currency account or settled into RMB through a licensed bank based on authentic trade and service documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, all export foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the customs declaration date, after which the exporter may retain the funds in a foreign exchange settlement account or sell them to a bank for RMB; there is no statutory mandatory conversion percentage under current SAFE deregulation. For service exports including AI data labeling BPO, the service fee remittance must be repatriated in accordance with the invoice and contract payment terms, generally within 90 days of service completion or sooner if the contract requires, and the bank must authenticate the transaction through the Service Trade, Foreign Exchange and Tax Payment/Collection Declaration Form before crediting or settling the proceeds.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) with delegated administration to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low. Global fintech EMIs such as Wise and PayPal are not licensed by the People's Bank of China or the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission to operate as commercial deposit-taking institutions inside mainland China and cannot open a compliant corporate settlement account for the WFOE. SAFE regulations require cross-border foreign exchange receipts and payments to be routed through designated domestic banks that perform authenticity and exact-duty review; therefore the WFOE cannot rely on a fintech EMI for inbound FDI, export proceeds collection, capital account settlement, or statutory tax remittances. Wise and PayPal may only be used as an ancillary offshore treasury layer for small-value disbursements to non-PRC vendors from the Hong Kong parent entity, but the mainland WFOE must retain a licensed Tier-1 bank as its principal clearing and liquidity rail.

More precisely, section 360A provides exemption from audit for a small private company; a private company qualifies as small in a financial year if it satisfies at least two of the following three criteria: (a) total revenue for the year not exceeding HKD 25 million; (b) total assets at the end of the year not exceeding HKD 20 million; (c) number of employees (all entities under the group) not exceeding 100. If the WFOE exceeds one of these thresholds? Actually, to be exempt, it must satisfy at least two of these criteria, i.e., a small private company that is not a member of a group of small private companies will be exempt only if it satisfies at least two of the following: revenue not exceeding HKD 25M, assets not exceeding HKD 20M, employees not exceeding 100. However, note that a company that is exempt still must maintain proper books and accounts and prepare financial statements for members. Also, a company that is not small must appoint a certified auditor. The mandatory audit regime therefore applies to: any WFOE that is part of a group and cannot claim small-group exemption, a WFOE that fails to satisfy at least two of the three size criteria, a WFOE that is not dormant and is required to file a profits tax return with certified financial statements, or a WFOE where a shareholder holding at least 5% of voting rights has served an audit notice.
