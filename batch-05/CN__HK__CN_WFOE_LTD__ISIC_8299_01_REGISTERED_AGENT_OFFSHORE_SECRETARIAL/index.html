Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; the online electronic portal is the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau), operated by the STA at the national and provincial levels. For Hong Kong SAR matters, the competent authority is the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of Hong Kong, which administers the Business Registration Office and the GovHK e-Tax electronic filing portal; however, the mainland China WFOE is primarily registered with the STA.

E-government filing portal

For the mainland China WFOE, the legal incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation automatically generates an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory tax identification number (TIN) for enterprises. Under the multi-license integration reform, the market regulator and the STA share registration data, so no separate tax registration certificate is issued. The WFOE must then log into the local e-Tax Bureau within 30 days of obtaining the business license to complete supplementary tax information, including the legal representative, financial officer, accounting standards, bank account details, and invoicing method. The WFOE must also register for the applicable Value-Added Tax status; general VAT taxpayer status is mandatory once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, and may be elected voluntarily below that threshold. For a foreign non-resident entity, such as a Hong Kong parent that has no physical establishment in mainland China but derives China-source income, the non-resident entity must undertake non-resident tax withholding registration with the STA through the e-Tax Bureau or the local STA service hall, using the foreign entity's business registration certificate, the relevant commercial contract, and payment evidence, and will be assigned a non-resident taxpayer identification number. If the Hong Kong jurisdiction entity itself requires registration, the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate and a Business Registration Number under the Business Registration Ordinance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the China WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise subject to Corporate Income Tax on its worldwide income at the statutory 25% rate and has no pass-through treatment. The Hong Kong ultimate shareholder may be subject to territorial-source taxation in Hong Kong, but that does not alter the mainland WFOE's entity-level residence status.

Available election forms

None; China does not operate a US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime. The WFOE may, however, elect simplified small-scale VAT taxpayer treatment while annual taxable turnover remains below RMB 5,000,000, or apply for general VAT taxpayer status; there is no elective consolidated group taxation and no domestic hybrid mismatch election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All mainland China entities must complete tax registration within 30 days of business establishment regardless of turnover; the mandatory annual taxable sales threshold for general VAT taxpayer registration is RMB 5,000,000. A WFOE below RMB 5,000,000 may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer at the reduced collection rate, but full general taxpayer status is required for export VAT zero-rating and refund claims in the ordinary course.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For exported goods, the 0% VAT rate applies when goods are physically exported from mainland China under the supervision of the General Administration of Customs and supported by a valid customs export declaration and actual foreign exchange receipt. For exported services, 0% applies to cross-border taxable services supplied to non-resident enterprises or individuals where the services are wholly consumed outside mainland China and fall within the STA-approved zero-rated cross-border services catalogue. A WFOE supplying offshore company secretarial or registered agent services to a Hong Kong or other non-resident client may claim zero-rating if the direct recipient is non-resident, the service is not physically consumed inside mainland China, and the WFOE retains adequate documentary proof of the offshore nature and foreign exchange settlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Release Bill / Export Goods Customs Declaration) with valid customs clearance sign-off
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export shipment from China
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the WFOE to the overseas buyer or service recipient
  • Packing List and executed sales or service contract describing the exported goods or supplied service
  • Bank SWIFT credit advice, Foreign Exchange Receipt Notice, and Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible foreign currency
  • Statutory export VAT invoice in the prescribed export invoice format
  • Additional certificates required by the importing jurisdiction, such as certificate of origin or non-preferential origin proof

The STA requires VAT invoices to be issued through the national Electronic Invoice Service Platform and the fully digitized electronic invoice system. Taxpayers use the statutory tax UKey or cloud-based e-invoicing software to generate Special VAT Invoices; each invoice carries a unique encrypted invoice number, a digital signature, and an anti-counterfeiting stamp validated in real-time by the STA through the Golden Tax Phase IV system. Invoice issuance data is transmitted through secured API channels, and the tax authority performs real-time clearance, quota control, and anomaly detection. The fully digital e-invoice mode enables taxpayers to issue, transmit, and store invoices without a traditional physical fiscal stamp, subject to STA authorization.

The STA and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange jointly audit export VAT zero-rating and refund claims. Where the supporting documentation is incomplete, unverified, forged, or fails to establish actual customs export and foreign exchange repatriation within the statutory deadline, the zero rate is denied, the tax refund is clawed back with late payment interest, and an administrative penalty of one to five times the fraudulent refund may be imposed under the Law on Tax Administration and Tax Collection of the People's Republic of China. The STA may also suspend the taxpayer's export tax refund qualification for a prescribed period. In cases involving intentional false export declarations or fabricated export documentation, the responsible entity and persons may face criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, including fines, imprisonment, and confiscation of property.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with conditional convertibility. Current account transactions are materially convertible: service-related receipts and payments, including statutory company secretarial fees, can be settled cross-border provided the underlying transaction is genuine and supported by contracts, commercial invoices and tax documentation. Capital account transactions remain strictly regulated. A WFOE's inbound equity capital injection is approved only after SAFE's foreign direct investment (FDI) registration, and the received foreign currency must be credited to a designated forex capital account. Conversion of the foreign currency capital into renminbi (CNY) is permitted on a real-transaction basis through the bank's settlement-awaiting payment account mechanics, but the funds may not be diverted to offshore loans, equity investments or securities trading. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is lawful only after audited financial statements, annual FDI compliance inspection, corporate income tax settlement, and withholding tax clearance are submitted to the remitting bank; the bank then verifies distributable reserves and files the remittance with SAFE. In practice, the bank reviews board resolutions, tax filing certificates (QFQ) and the audit report for consistency, so remittance is not automatic. Any mismatch between the declared paid-in capital and actual operating expenditure triggers an enhanced documentary review, and PBOC retains macro-prudential authority to delay settlement during stress episodes. Convertibility therefore exists in law but is operationally gated by real-business verification, making robust documentary discipline and a high-quality Tier-1 banking relationship indispensable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory mandatory foreign-exchange surrender system was abolished, so there is no fixed conversion ratio for export receipts. However, SAFE requires that all export and service trade proceeds be repatriated to China and credited to the company's domestic foreign-exchange account or directly settled into CNY within the goods and service trade collection monitoring window, which for service exports is generally not later than 90 days from the service completion date. Failure to repatriate on time requires a deferred receipt declaration. For this WFOE, revenues from secretarial and registered-agent services rendered to overseas clients must be matched against signed engagements, bank statements and safekept supporting evidence, and reported into the SAFE digital monitoring platform for trade and service-fee income before any subsequent outbound disbursement.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and its provincial branches

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Non-viable and legally prohibited as a primary banking rail. Offshore digital EMI firms such as Revolut, Airwallex, Currenxie or London/HK-licensed payment institutions are not commercial banks recognized by PBOC or SAFE for FDI capital account operations. Chinese banking regulations require that a WFOE's capital injection, profit repatriation, tax withholding payments and SAFE filing obligations be executed by a licensed bank inside the People's Republic of China. An offshore EMI cannot obtain the required SAFE business registration code, cannot open a capital account that links to SAFE's capital project information system, and cannot legally convert foreign currency capital into CNY for domestic expenditure. Using an EMI for the main treasury route would expose the WFOE to unlicensed cross-border payment enforcement, freeze of flow channels, and adverse compliance determinations. At best, an EMI may be used by the Hong Kong parent merely as an internal pre-funding conduit before remitting to China via a licensed bank, but that introduces inexplicable transaction layers and should be avoided. The only acceptable treasury architecture is to place all cross-border capital movements through Tier-1 domestic commercial banks with direct SAFE connectivity and experienced international settlement desks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity contributions must follow the SAFE FDI registration pathway. Step one: after SAMR approval of the WFOE articles, the company completes SAFE FDI basic registration through the designated bank to obtain an FDI business reference code. Step two: the bank opens a foreign exchange capital account and a CNY basic deposit account, linking both to the SAFE capital project information system. Step three: the Hong Kong parent remits registered capital via SWIFT MT103 using transaction purpose code 708 (direct investment inward remittance) and remittance information stating 'registered capital contribution to [WFOE name]', quoting the SAFE FDI registration number, the investor's offshore bank account and the investment amount confirmed by the capital verification or bank inflow certificate. Step four: upon receipt, the bank conducts prudence verification against the business license, the FDI registration certificate and the shareholder resolution, credits the foreign currency to the capital account, and then, where needed, converts to CNY under the settlement-awaiting payment account mechanism with SAFE three-in-one electronic filing. Capital must not be applied to prohibited uses such as repayments on non-export related lending, securities purchases or entrusted loans; every CNY disbursement from the settlement-awaiting account must cite an authentic trade or expense background, with corporate expense vouchers, supplier contracts and fapiao (official tax invoices) preserved for at least ten years.Outbound clearing for the WFOE will principally travel over SWIFT MT103 for standard non-trade service payments such as subscriptions, technology fees, secretarial sub-contracting fees and intercompany settlements, and over trade finance instruments only where the WFOE actually imports goods or equipment. For genuine merchandise imports, a sight commercial letter of credit issued by the domestic bank in favor of the Hong Kong supplier is the preferred rail, with the L/C application supported by the import contract and the import customs declaration; documentary collections (D/P and D/A) are permissible but less liquid and less favorable to the payee. For service disbursements, each MT103 transmitted by the bank must transmit a valid transaction purpose code corresponding to the current account service category, and the bank simultaneously performs real-trade verification of invoice, contract, proof of service delivery and tax withholding certificate when payment is made to a related party in Hong Kong. Payments to the Hong Kong parent under a management fee or royalty arrangement require an arm's length transfer pricing report and the domestic resident withholding tax voucher at the 5% or 10% treaty-reduced rate, otherwise the bank cannot release the cross-border instruction.Real-demand-oriented onshore CNY forward settlement and sale contracts (forward contracts executed with a licensed domestic bank against actual service receipts or payments, with invoice and contract evidence filed at trade date)Cross-currency swaps with a genuine underlying interest or cash-flow exposure, such as where capital is held in USD and converted periodically into CNY for operating expenses, provided the swap is front-ended by a licensed bank and documented under SAFE's real-need principleFX structural products directly linked to recognized trade flows may be used but only on a non-leveraged basis and without embedded option selling positions that convert into naked speculative exposureNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for the onshore WFOE because SAFE prohibits domestic entities from transacting offshore NDFs in CNY; the Hong Kong parent could theoretically do a CNH NDF offshore with a bank outside China, but that would not be a WFOE clearing rail and is disrecommended due to operational and documentation complexity, and any such hedge would not be recognized by the mainland bank for margin purposes

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Non-viable and legally prohibited as a primary banking rail. Offshore digital EMI firms such as Revolut, Airwallex, Currenxie or London/HK-licensed payment institutions are not commercial banks recognized by PBOC or SAFE for FDI capital account operations. Chinese banking regulations require that a WFOE's capital injection, profit repatriation, tax withholding payments and SAFE filing obligations be executed by a licensed bank inside the People's Republic of China. An offshore EMI cannot obtain the required SAFE business registration code, cannot open a capital account that links to SAFE's capital project information system, and cannot legally convert foreign currency capital into CNY for domestic expenditure. Using an EMI for the main treasury route would expose the WFOE to unlicensed cross-border payment enforcement, freeze of flow channels, and adverse compliance determinations. At best, an EMI may be used by the Hong Kong parent merely as an internal pre-funding conduit before remitting to China via a licensed bank, but that introduces inexplicable transaction layers and should be avoided. The only acceptable treasury architecture is to place all cross-border capital movements through Tier-1 domestic commercial banks with direct SAFE connectivity and experienced international settlement desks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 40 million in aggregate for other related-party transactions, RMB 200 million for tangible property transactions, RMB 10 million for intangible property transfers, and RMB 100 million for financing arrangements; Master File required for annual related-party transaction turnover exceeding RMB 1 billion. Note: if a WFOE has such thresholds, it must prepare the China Local File irrespective of the global threshold.

Economic substance scope: Under PRC GAAR (CIT Law Article 47), Special Tax Adjustment Rules, and the Beneficial Ownership requirements of SAT Announcement 9/2018, a WFOE must demonstrate real functions, assets, and risks (CIGA) attributable to income. Registered-agent activities must be physically performed in China; the HK offshore arm cannot claim those functions. An entity with no employees, no assets, or no operating substance is recharacterized as a shell, and treaty benefits are denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Article 201 of the Criminal Law of the PRC (tax evasion) - monetary fine of 1-5 times the evaded amount plus confiscation; Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law imposes 50% to 5 times penalty; VAT counterfeit invoice fraud is further punishable under Criminal Law Article 205 (up to life imprisonment).

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives are jointly and severally liable for tax arrears and surcharges; they face criminal detention of 3-7 years, asset freezing, blacklisting by market supervision and civil court enforcement, travel bans (exit denial) under TCAL Article 44, and disqualification from directorships for up to 10 years.
Transfer Pricing / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: PRC CIT Law Article 41; SAT Notice 42/2016 Special Tax Adjustment measures; Article 121 TCAL: additional interest (5% per annum), 50%-5x penalty for resulting underpaid tax; denial of treaty relief under SAT Announcement 35/2015 and 9/2018.

Director personal liability: Piercing the veil under PRC Company Law Article 20: shareholders/directors who abuse limited liability and the corporate entity to evade tax will be personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; they may be subject to criminal fraud (Criminal Law Article 266), personal asset confiscation, and a ban on leaving the country (the List of Violating Taxpayers may be blacklisted by the tax and immigration authorities).
Money Laundering / Shell-company AML Deficiency

Statutory sanction: PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law (2022 Amendment) Article 55: fines up to RMB 2 million per violation; illegal business license revocation; criminal under Criminal Law Article 191: imprisonment of 5-10 years and fines if the WFOE is used as a laundering conduit.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE are subject to criminal liability as money-laundering principals or accomplices; personal and family assets are subject to freezing/confiscation; travel bans and Interpol red notices; a HK court judgment may be enforced through mutual legal assistance; life-time exclusion from being a director or compliance officer in regulated sectors.
