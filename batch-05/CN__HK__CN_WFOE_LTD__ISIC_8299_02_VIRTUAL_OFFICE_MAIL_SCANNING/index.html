Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a private company limited by shares, and no blocked escrow account or capital-holding account is required at incorporation. The Articles of Association may provide for an authorized share capital of HKD 10,000 divided into 10,000 ordinary shares of HKD 1.00 each, issued at par as fully paid upon allotment without verification of capital funding.

Funding & unlock path: Office premises require a Fire Safety Certificate or Fire Service Installations status under the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95) and the Fire Safety (Commercial Premises) Ordinance (Cap. 502), certifying smoke detection, emergency lighting and exit signage; a Buildings Department compliance letter or an Authorized Person's certificate certifies structural load and permitted office use; the Labour Department's Occupational Safety and Health Regulation requires an electrical safety and working-space inspection for staff; dangerous goods quantities must not exceed exempt storage limits. Because the operation is mail scanning in a virtual office, no Civil Defence site licensing or dangerous-goods licence is triggered.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); the WFOE is registered and files in mainland China, not in the Hong Kong SAR. Online filing and registration are conducted through the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau (etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and supplementary local electronic tax services administered by provincial and municipal STA branches. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department has jurisdiction only over the non-resident Hong Kong shareholder.

E-government filing portal

For the newly formed PRC WFOE: (1) obtain a business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) under the multi-certificate integration system; the automatically issued 18-character Unified Social Credit Code is the sole taxpayer identification number and replaces a separate tax registration certificate; (2) SAMR transmits the incorporation data to the STA, upon which the WFOE must complete the taxpayer registration recordal and tax-attribute setup in the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau, including legal representatives, responsible person, finance and accounting personnel, registered office, capital injection details, bank deposit account, VAT payer type, withholding-agent registration for individual income tax, and stamp-tax registration; (3) file the Foreign Investment Information Report with SAMR because the 100 percent shareholder is a Hong Kong SAR entity; (4) if the WFOE will claim treaty benefits on dividends, interest or royalties payable to the Hong Kong shareholder, obtain and submit a Hong Kong tax-residence certificate to the STA to support the reduced rate under the Mainland and Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement; (5) if the HK shareholder itself is a non-resident enterprise earning China-source income without a fixed place, it must separately apply for a Non-resident Enterprise Taxpayer Identification Number through withholding registration by the Chinese payer in the electronic tax bureau; (6) open a basic bank deposit account and file the account number with the tax authority; and (7) activate the e-invoice quota and VAT return modules in the electronic tax bureau. There is no minimum capital requirement for the WFOE under the current PRC Foreign Investment Law, but registered capital must be declared and, if contributed in foreign currency, handled and reported according to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

None for entity classification; PRC law does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All PRC business registrants are tax-registered from incorporation without any turnover threshold. For VAT classification, an entity is a small-scale taxpayer if annual taxable turnover is not more than RMB 5,000,000; once annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer applies. Small-scale taxpayers with monthly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 are currently exempt from VAT under the continuing exemption policy.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim VAT zero-rating for a qualifying cross-border service (virtual office, mail receipt, scanning, document digitization and related business-process outsourcing) if the service is supplied to an offshore customer, the service is wholly consumed outside mainland China, the service falls within the STA-published zero-rate/exemption catalogue for cross-border taxable services, the recipient is not a mainland Chinese individual or enterprise, and the foreign currency payment is received from an overseas bank account. The WFOE must evidence that no part of the service is rendered to or for the benefit of a mainland China taxable person and that the direct beneficiary is the overseas customer. If the service is not within the zero-rate catalogue, it may still qualify for VAT exemption, but exemption does not attract an input VAT credit.

  • Signed cross-border service contract and the commercial invoice issued in the customer's name
  • Original or bank-certified foreign exchange collection receipt (SAFE cross-border foreign exchange receipt declaration and bank inward remittance advice) proving the service proceeds were repatriated to the WFOE's PRC bank account from the HK or offshore customer
  • Overseas customer business registration certificate or tax-residence certificate, or other independent confirmation of the customer's offshore status
  • Service consumption evidence such as email instructions, mail scanning records, service performance logs and digital delivery metadata showing the service was rendered and received outside mainland China
  • If physical mail or goods cross a PRC border, the PRC Customs Export Declaration and the transport document (Bill of Lading or Air Waybill) are additionally required

The PRC requires VAT invoices, including the new Full Digital Electronic Invoices, to be generated through the National Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform; invoice data are transmitted in near-real time to the STA and are secured by a unique tax-control serial number, electronic signature and encrypted anti-fraud code. Legacy tax-control invoices are issued from a qualified tax-control device that must report completed invoice data to the tax authority. Every VAT invoice must be verified using the nationwide invoice verification platform before the purchaser may take an input VAT credit. Cross-border zero-rated supplies must be declared monthly, and the export-tax-refund, e-invoice and foreign exchange collection data are cross-checked by the STA.

The STA and SAFE cross-check the zero-rated declaration, e-invoice, service contract, customs data and the foreign exchange repatriation record. If the claimed zero-rating is disallowed, the WFOE must pay the output VAT on the service at 6 percent (or 13 percent if recharacterized as a supply of goods), plus a late payment surcharge of 0.05 percent per day and an administrative penalty of between 50 percent and five times the tax undercharged. Where the zero-rating claim is supported by false contracts, false foreign exchange receipts or fabricated export records, the STA may suspend future tax refunds, recover any paid refund, and refer the matter for criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds under PRC law. Under foreign-exchange rules, failure to repatriate export proceeds or false forex declaration may expose the WFOE to SAFE fines and compulsory repatriation orders.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is convertible on the current account for trade and service transactions, but the capital account remains heavily regulated. The WFOE may freely settle or retain export/service revenue through a current-account foreign exchange settlement account at a designated bank, provided the transaction has genuine underlying business documentation and is filed in SAFE's trade monitoring system. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is treated as a current-account item under dividend distribution; once the annual financial statements are audited by a PRC CPA, corporate income tax is settled, and the bank verifies the board resolution and tax payment proof, the remittance can be executed through a designated bank without prior SAFE approval. However, capital account inflows such as equity injection, shareholder loans, external debt, and cross-border guarantees require registration with SAFE or its delegated banks and are subject to strict purpose limitations. Funds converted from capital accounts cannot be diverted to non-operating uses, securities, or entrusted loans. Profit recoupment through offshore cost transfers is scrutinized; transfer pricing documentation may be required. Any virtual-office and mail-scanning revenue must be supported by service contracts, client identity files, and transaction-level reconciliations to satisfy SAFE's genuine business and anti-money-laundering reviews.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE regulations, there is no mandatory conversion ratio for current-account export proceeds; the WFOE may retain 100% of its export or service receipts in foreign currency or sell them to designated banks at market rates. Goods-export proceeds must generally be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration and reported through the trade verification system. Service-export proceeds, including virtual-office and mail-scanning fees, must be collected according to the underlying contract terms and reported to SAFE in a timely manner; any prolonged non-collection must be explained with supporting evidence. Receipts cannot remain indefinitely in offshore accounts unless SAFE has approved a centralized treasury arrangement. All conversions between CNY and foreign currency must be executed through a licensed bank with a genuine payment reason and supporting documentation.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Not viable as a primary onshore treasury rail. Offshore digital EMI platforms, including Airwallex, Payoneer, and Stripe Treasury-style arrangements, are not licensed banks in mainland China and are legally barred from opening domestic CNY current accounts, accepting deposits, or settling trade payments on behalf of the WFOE without a licensed onshore bank sponsor. They may operate only as auxiliary payment facilitators for inbound foreign-currency collections, with funds ultimately required to flow through a domestic Tier-1 bank and to be reported to SAFE. Using an unlicensed EMI as the principal settlement agent would violate PBOC payment-licensing rules and SAFE reporting obligations; therefore, statutory capital injection, capital verification, tax remittance, dividend outbound payment, and FX hedging must all be executed through a licensed onshore commercial bank.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Every Hong Kong incorporated company must appoint an independent certified public accountant (practising) as auditor and have financial statements audited annually, regardless of balance sheet size, turnover, or headcount. There is no quantitative exemption for small or medium private companies. The only statutory exception is a dormant company that has no significant accounting transactions during the relevant period and has passed the required resolution. For a licensed trust or company service provider operating a virtual office and mail scanning CMRA service, the statutory financial statement audit remains mandatory.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding tax on distributions from a Chinese resident enterprise to its non-resident enterprise shareholder; reduced to 5% under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement if the Hong Kong shareholder is the beneficial owner and has held at least 25% of the capital throughout the 365 days preceding the dividend distribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Substantive Local File threshold: RMB 40 million in 'other related-party transactions' (including service fees and management fees); RMB 200 million for related-party tangible-asset transfers; RMB 100 million for related-party intangible-asset transfers; RMB 100 million for related-party financial-asset transfers. The Master File threshold is annual related-party revenue of RMB 2 billion. Even below these thresholds, a country-by-country report and Contemporaneous TP Documentation may be demanded where the anti-avoidance risk assessment is triggered by a low-margin mail-scanning/CMRA business.

Economic substance scope: China's corporate income tax law applies a substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (CIT Law Article 47) to recharacterize arrangements with no reasonable commercial purpose. For a WFOE performing virtual-office and mail-scanning, the relevant Core Income-Generating Activities in China include client contracting, service performance, invoice issuance, cash collection, staff supervision, and risk assumption; if all those activities are conducted by the Hong Kong parent and the WFOE is only a mailbox/letter-box, the Chinese tax authority may deny the entity's profit attribution, disallow deductions and treaty withholding benefits, and reallocate profits to the actual function performers. The Hong Kong parent must also satisfy Hong Kong ESR economic-substance requirements to maintain its residency certificate for DTA purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Falsified Invoices, Artificial Costs and Hidden Revenue

Statutory sanction: TCAL Article 63: 50%-500% penalty on the underpaid tax plus daily late surcharge; PRC Criminal Law Article 205 imposes criminal liability for fictitious VAT invoices with fines and imprisonment up to life; Criminal Law Article 201 punishes tax evasion with fines and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Upon piercing of the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Article 23, the director and controlling shareholder are jointly and severally liable for tax debts and restitution; personal assets may be attached after corporate assets are insufficient. The individual is entered in the tax blacklist, triggering exit bans, denial of high-consumption facilities, financial-market restrictions, and cross-border travel controls; criminal proceedings may impose imprisonment on the directly responsible director.
Transfer Pricing and Hollow Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 41 and 48 tax authority may make a special tax adjustment within six years and recharacterize income; interest is charged at the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points; Announcement 2016 No. 42 documentation failure penalties range from RMB 5,000 to RMB 100,000 with an adverse shift in burden of proof; CIT Law Article 47 GAAR may disregard the entire WFOE if it lacks commercial substance.

Director personal liability: The director can be held personally liable for the tax shortfall if the shell-company structure is used to abuse the corporate form; PRC Company Law Article 23 allows creditors, including the tax authority as a creditor, to pierce the veil and claim from the shareholder's personal assets; the director is blacklisted and subject to exit/travel bans; the artificial profit shift may be prosecuted as tax evasion, leading to criminal imprisonment and fines for the director as the responsible person.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Abusive DTA/Beneficial Ownership Claim

Statutory sanction: TCAL Article 69 imposes a 50%-300% penalty on the withholding agent for the un-withheld amount; CIT Law Article 37 and Article 38 require the WFOE to withhold; under Announcement 2015 No.60 and Announcement 2019 No.35 the treaty benefit is denied and the additional 5% tax is collected with interest, and conduit arrangements are disregarded.

Director personal liability: The director who authorized the unlawful distribution is a directly responsible officer and can be jointly liable for the withheld tax and penalties; the corporate veil is pierced under Company Law Article 23 where the shareholder treats the WFOE as a tool to evade tax; the director is exposed to tax blacklist measures, travel restrictions, and exit bans; if the non-withholding is intentional and combined with false documentation, Criminal Law Article 201 criminal prosecutions may lead to imprisonment and confiscation of personal assets.
