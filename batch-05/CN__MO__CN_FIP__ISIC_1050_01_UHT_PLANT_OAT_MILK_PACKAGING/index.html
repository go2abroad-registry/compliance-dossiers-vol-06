Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Macao law imposes no minimum paid-up capital for partnerships. However, partners must contribute capital as agreed in the partnership deed, and contributions may be in cash, kind, or services. No escrow or blocked account requirement applies.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Fire Services Bureau (CB) - requires installation of fire alarms, extinguishers, and emergency exits; structural load inspection from the Land, Public and Transport Bureau (DSSOPT) to ensure the building can support heavy machinery; Occupational Safety and Health (OSH) approval from the Labour Affairs Bureau (DSAL) - requires a workplace safety audit, employee training records, and compliance with the OSH Law (Law No. 4/2019).

Paid-in capital rule: None. Macao law imposes no minimum paid-up capital for partnerships. However, partners must contribute capital as agreed in the partnership deed, and contributions may be in cash, kind, or services. No escrow or blocked account requirement applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Not_Applicable_No_VAT_GST (Macao levies selective Consumer Tax and Tourism Tax but no general VAT/GST or Sales and Use Tax)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST turnover registration threshold exists because Macao does not impose VAT/GST. If the partnership imports raw materials or finished goods subject to Macao Consumer Tax (Imposto de Consumo), it must register with Macao Customs as the importer of record and settle the tax at clearance. Tourism Tax registration is only required if the entity also operates a hotel, restaurant, or equivalent tourism establishment, which is not the case for a UHT milk manufacturing plant.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable: Macao has no VAT zero-rating mechanism because exports are not within the scope of any Macao indirect tax. Exports of aseptic UHT oat milk and packaging from the Macao plant are outside Macao Consumer Tax unless the goods are specifically listed for internal consumption, and no output VAT is charged. If the goods are cleared from Macao into mainland China, they are subject to mainland China VAT at importation under the Mainland and Macao Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement (CEPA) or ordinary customs procedures; any VAT refund or exemption on the mainland side belongs to the mainland importer and must be substantiated under China customs and tax rules.

The partnership must issue invoices that satisfy the Macao Commercial Code and DSF documentary requirements, and may use DSF electronic services for tax returns and payments; no government real-time clearance of every invoice is required.

Because Macao does not operate an export VAT refund scheme, there is no explicit VAT clawback. However, Macao Customs and DSF may audit the documentary chain of export declarations, transfer pricing, and underlying commercial substance. False, fraudulent, or unverifiable export declarations can expose the partnership and its partners to customs contravention penalties, suspension or revocation of the Macao industrial licence, denial of CEPA tariff preferences, and criminal sanctions for smuggling under Macao foreign-trade law. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Macao Special Administrative Region operates a fully convertible open foreign-exchange system with no Exchange Control Ordinance or mandatory surrender regime. The Macao Pataca is pegged to the Hong Kong dollar at approximately MOP 1.03 per HKD and trades within a de facto band against the USD via Hong Kong linked exchange rate. Current account payments, trade settlements, repatriation of profits and intercompany service fees carry no AMCM preapproval; Macao banks may debit or credit MOP, HKD, CNY, USD and other freely convertible currencies for the FIP. The main friction is the mainland China border: if the Macao FIP remits paid-in capital into a mainland manufacturing sub-entity or receives export proceeds from a mainland UHT plant, PRC rules require SAFE registration, FDI capital account admission for capital items and tax-cleared repatriation for current-account profits. Capital account conversion of RMB remains controlled; current and capital accounts are segregated. Profit remittances from mainland require audited PRC financials, corporate income tax filing proof, beneficiary business certificate, and no outstanding tax or SAFE violation. No Macao-side remittance tax is imposed on outbound dividends, although home jurisdiction tax reporting remains.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Macao law imposes no surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio; export proceeds may be retained in MOP, HKD, USD or any freely convertible currency. For mainland PRC exporting operations tied to the FIP, SAFE requires export foreign-currency proceeds to be repatriated to a domestic foreign-currency current account; voluntary settlement at market rate applies, with no fixed percentage ratio. Any collection maturity exceeding 90 days from the customs export declaration date must be supported by a delay-collection explanation and registered in SAFE's goods-trade monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: Monetary Authority of Macao (AMCM), the de facto central bank and foreign-exchange supervisor of the Macao Special Administrative Region; the People's Republic of China's State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) remains the enforcement authority for mainland-facing RMB/FX capital and current account controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as primary treasury. Offshore fintech EMIs are generally not licensed by AMCM as credit institutions and cannot hold a legal Macao deposit-taking mandate, clear MOP/HKD cheques, or open a SWIFT-enabled corporate account for a Macao FIP. AMCM reserves deposit-taking and payment clearing to licensed banks and payment institutions under the Macao Financial System Act. Mainland Chinese foreign-exchange controls also prohibit unlicensed foreign EMIs from servicing cross-border RMB flows. Feasibility rating is Low-to-Nil; the FIP should bank exclusively through a licensed Tier 1 Macao bank and may consider an EMI only as a non-operational expense card where the EMI holds a Hong Kong or Singapore license and the underlying accounting is separately reconciled.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital enters only through the FIP's dedicated bank account at an AMCM-licensed bank. The remitter uses SWIFT MT103 with complete banking identifiers; in the remittance information field, state the purpose as cross-border capital injection and reference the SAFE or AMCM-mapped FDI paid-in capital purpose code. For mainland China inbound transfers, the receiving/remitting bank must complete SAFE cross-border reporting and the foreign exchange settlement application must use the registered FDI capital account purpose code, not a current-account trade code. Upon receipt, an appointed certified public accountant conducts capital verification, signs the capital verification report, and confirms that the proceeds were not mixed with trade receivables or operating cash. No settlement through personal accounts, ATM transactions, prepaid cards, crypto rails or third-party intermediary accounts is permitted.Use direct SWIFT MT103 and MT202 COV for ordinary raw-material payments such as hydrolyzed oats, packaging laminates and flavoring supplies, including the applicable goods-trade purpose code, invoice number, contract and customs reference. For high-value UHT sterilizer and aseptic filling equipment, issue an irrevocable documentary letter of credit via SWIFT MT700, amend through MT707 and settle with MT754/MT709 under UCP600 and ISBP745 rules. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable as primary treasury. Offshore fintech EMIs are generally not licensed by AMCM as credit institutions and cannot hold a legal Macao deposit-taking mandate, clear MOP/HKD cheques, or open a SWIFT-enabled corporate account for a Macao FIP. AMCM reserves deposit-taking and payment clearing to licensed banks and payment institutions under the Macao Financial System Act. Mainland Chinese foreign-exchange controls also prohibit unlicensed foreign EMIs from servicing cross-border RMB flows. Feasibility rating is Low-to-Nil; the FIP should bank exclusively through a licensed Tier 1 Macao bank and may consider an EMI only as a non-operational expense card where the EMI holds a Hong Kong or Singapore license and the underlying accounting is separately reconciled.
