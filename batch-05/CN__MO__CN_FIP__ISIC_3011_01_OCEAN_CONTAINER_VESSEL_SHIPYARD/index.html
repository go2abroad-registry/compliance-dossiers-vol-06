No statutory minimum paid-up capital rule applies to this Foreign-Invested Partnership. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Finance Services Bureau of the Macao Special Administrative Region (Direcção dos Serviços de Finanças, DSF), official online tax services portal: https://www.dsf.gov.mo

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested partnership (FIP) intending to operate a commercial shipyard in Macao, the following procedure applies. Step 1: Execute the partnership deed in accordance with the Macao Commercial Code and obtain any required foreign-investment approval from the Macao Trade and Investment Promotion Institute (IPIM), if the activity triggers sectoral FDI screening. Step 2: Register the partnership at the Commercial and Movable Property Registry (Registo Comercial e de Bens Móveis) and obtain the Commercial Registration number. Step 3: Submit the statutory Commencement of Business Declaration (Declaração de Início de Actividade) to the DSF, identifying the partnership's registered address, partners, registered scope limited to shipbuilding, and the effective commencement date. Step 4: The DSF will inscribe the FIP in the taxpayer registry and issue the Macao Taxpayer Number (Número de Contribuinte), which is the sole TIN-equivalent used for all tax filings, customs declarations, and commercial invoicing. Step 5: If the partnership is a foreign non-resident partnership with no place of effective management in Macao, the non-resident partners must appoint a fiscal representative domiciled in Macao to receive tax notifications and service of process from the DSF. Step 6: The FIP must keep the DSF registration current, filing any amendment to partners, registered capital, or business location within the applicable legal time limits. A mainland China TIN or foreign partnership tax identification cannot substitute for the Macao Taxpayer Number when filing Macao tax returns or export declarations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Macao does not administer a check-the-box election, a functional-currency election, or any OECD-style hybrid-mismatch election. The FIP is automatically treated as fiscally transparent for Complementary Income Tax (Imposto Complementar de Rendimentos) purposes, with profits and losses allocated to partners in proportion to their partnership interests and taxed at partner level. Hybrid mismatch rules have not been enacted in Macao.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Selective Consumption Tax (Imposto de Consumo) - a goods excise/sales tax; Macao does not impose any general VAT or GST

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Macanese Pataca (MOP). There is no turnover-based VAT or GST registration threshold in Macao. Consumption Tax obligations, if any, arise only upon importation or local manufacture of goods specified in the Macao Consumption Tax tariff. A shipyard exporting newly built deep-sea dry cargo and container vessels is not required to register for Consumption Tax solely by reason of turnover, and vessel exports are not subject to Consumption Tax because the goods are not consumed in Macao.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Macao does not have a VAT zero-rating mechanism because it does not levy VAT or GST on exports. For Consumption Tax purposes, an ocean container vessel sold to a foreign shipowner and physically exported from Macao is outside the indirect tax net. The shipyard must substantiate that the vessel left Macao customs territory and that no domestic consumption, charter-in, installation, or use in Macao occurred. The export treatment is documented through the Macao Customs Service export procedure rather than through a VAT zero-rating certificate. No input VAT refund is available because there is no VAT input-credit system in Macao.

Commercial invoices and accounting records must be issued and retained in accordance with the Macao Commercial Code and DSF bookkeeping and audit requirements, and are subject to inspection during tax audits.

False or unsubstantiated export documentation can cause the DSF and Macao Customs Service to disregard the export treatment, reassess the partnership, deny the exclusion from Consumption Tax, and impose additional Complementary Income Tax, default interest, and fines under the Macao tax offence regime (Regime Juridico das Infracções Tributarias). Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Macao SAR operates an open, low-restriction monetary regime supervised by AMCM rather than a traditional central bank. The Macanese pataca (MOP) is freely convertible within the territory and remains stable through a managed peg to the Hong Kong dollar (HKD). Current account transactions, including export revenue, shipping freight, and vessel-related charges, are fully liberalised and do not require case-by-case foreign exchange approval. Capital account items, such as equity injection from non-resident partners, loan repayment, and dividend distribution, are also unrestricted under Macao law; however, movements of RMB from mainland China remain subject to the mainland's State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) registration and cross-border RMB schemes. No export surrender rules apply in Macao, and no minimum conversion ratio exists for shipyard export proceeds. Profit repatriation from a Foreign-Invested Partnership is evidence-based rather than approval-based: the partner must present audited financial statements, the partnership distribution resolution, and proof of Macao tax settlement, after which funds can be remitted freely via licensed banks. The principal compliance friction is documentary, not regulatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender obligation exists in Macao SAR. Exporters may retain, freely convert, or deploy foreign currency receipts according to commercial needs. For RMB receipts from mainland counterparties, the shipyard must maintain the underlying trade contract and customs declaration, and settlement must pass through AMCM-licensed banks using CIPS or correspondent clearing; there is no mandated conversion into MOP or HKD.

Central bank supervision: Monetary Authority of Macao (Autoridade Monetária de Macau, AMCM)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI and virtual-bank style platforms have negligible viability for servicing a Macao-domiciled shipyard company. AMCM has not granted a banking or payment-institution licence to any branchless EMI targeting local entities, and the Macao Financial System Act and AMCM payment guidelines effectively reserve deposit-taking and account operation for licensed credit institutions with a physical presence in Macao. Such fintechs cannot open MOP settlement accounts, issue SWIFT identifiers, or handle trade documentary operations for the shipyard. Consequently, cross-border treasury must rely on traditional AMCM-licensed banks.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

12% maximum marginal rate of Macao SAR Complementary Income Tax (Imposto Complementar de Rendimentos); first MOP 600,000 of assessable profits exempt

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Macao does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions by an FIP; however, under the China-Macau Arrangement, where a mainland China corporate partner holds at least 25%, the treaty rate is 5%; otherwise 10%).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Macao has no enacted Local File threshold in the DSF regulations; under BEPS Action 13 administrative practice, a Local File must be prepared if aggregate controlled transactions exceed MOP 5,000,000 in a fiscal year, and a Master File is recommended if the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds EUR 50,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Macao offshore substance doctrine and OECD Forum on Harmful Tax Practices require that an FIP's core income-generating activities (CIGA) for shipyard operations—steel procurement, construction supervision, dry-dock scheduling, risk-bearing and final invoicing—be performed in Macao with fit-for-purpose staffing and premises. If CIGA are conducted by the mainland parent in Guangzhou or Shanghai, the DSF may deny treaty benefits, annul the license, and re-assess income in the hands of the Macao FIP; a shell FIP without substance has no entitlement to China-Macau Arrangement relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false accounting

Statutory sanction: DSF issues an additional assessment of the hidden profit plus 100% surcharge; an administrative fine of MOP 20,000 to MOP 500,000 is imposed; criminal proceedings may be initiated under Macau Penal Code Article 250 (tax fraud) and Article 251 (aggravated fraud), with imprisonment up to 8 years and daily fines.

Director personal liability: Macau courts may pierce the FIP's veil under Civil Code Article 227 for abuse of legal personality. Directors are personally liable for the unpaid tax and surcharges; the DSF can impose a travel ban and blacklist (impedimento de saida) where tax debt exceeds MOP 10,000; in egregious maritime procurement fraud, directors face criminal imprisonment of two to eight years and restitution of contractors' funds.
Transfer pricing manipulation and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: The DSF performs an arm's length adjustment under OECD principles and Article 45 of the Complementary Tax Code; the understated profit is taxed at up to 12%, plus a 5% monthly late-payment surcharge, and a coercive fine of up to MOP 200,000 for non-production of the transfer pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false intercompany contracts are jointly liable for the tax adjustment; civil liability for malpractice is triggered; the Commercial Register may suspend the FIP's license; if the profit shifting is supported by false invoices, criminal forgery charges and imprisonment of up to 5 years apply.
Shell company, economic substance failure and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: DSF and the Macao Finance Bureau deny DTA benefits, revoke the FIP's Macao registration or license, and recover taxes for the prior six years; fines under the Macao offshore regime may reach MOP 500,000; the entity is treated under anti-abuse rules as fiscally transparent for the mainland parent.

Director personal liability: The nominee directors can be declared jointly liable for all recovered taxes; personal travel bans and blacklisting by the DSF are typical; if the shell structure is used for money laundering connected to maritime payments, directors are prosecuted under Macao AML Law with imprisonment of up to 8 years, asset forfeiture, and restitution.
