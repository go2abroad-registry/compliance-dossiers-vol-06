Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Commercial and Property Registry of Macao (Registo Comercial e de Bens Móveis), Macao Special Administrative Region, supervised by the Legal and Judicial Affairs Bureau (Direcção dos Serviços de Assuntos de Justiça, DSAJ).

Governing statute

Macao Special Administrative Region Commercial Code (Código Comercial) approved by Decree-Law No. 40/99/M of 3 August 1999, as amended, Articles 61-99, 174-233 and 234-261; Commercial Registration Code (Código de Registo Comercial) approved by Decree-Law No. 56/99/M of 21 September 1999, as amended, Articles 2, 7, 12, 18, 27 and 31; and Decree-Law No. 58/99/M of 21 October 1999, as amended, on Macao offshore institutions insofar as it permits a foreign-invested limited partnership to use pass-through treatment.

Formation / constitutional filings

Formal application for commercial registration (requisição de registo) for the constitution of a Foreign-Invested Partnership, accompanied by the notarized Partnership Deed (Contrato de Sociedade), the First Board Resolution, the registered office proof, and good-standing certificates of all partners, filed at the Commercial and Property Registry.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 34 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Foreign-Invested Partnership can be formed without any statutory minimum paid-up capital under the Macao Commercial Code. The Partnership Deed must state the subscribed capital, the form of each contribution, and the payment timeline; the contribution may be cash or in-kind. No blocked escrow account or bank certificate is required for partnership registration. If a limited partnership structure is adopted, at least one general partner must have unlimited joint liability, while each limited partner's exposure is limited to the agreed capital contribution. In-kind contributions require a sworn valuation statement in the deed.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Macao Financial Services Bureau (Direcção dos Serviços de Finanças, DSF), online electronic portal: https://www.dsf.gov.mo. Customs and external-trade registration is administered by the Macao Customs Service (Autoridade Alfandegária de Macau) through the Macao Single Window for trade declarations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Form the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) under the Macao Commercial Code and register with the Commercial and Movable Property Registry (Conservatório do Registo Comercial e de Bens Móveis); obtain the commercial registration certificate and commercial registration number. Step 2: Apply to the Macao Financial Services Bureau for the Taxpayer Number (Número de Contribuinte), using the DSF taxpayer registration service; submit the commercial registration certificate, partnership deed, proof of registered office in Macao, the foreign investor's notarized entity formation documents or valid passport, and identification of all partners and local managers. Step 3: If the FIP will carry on cross-border trade in collectible trading cards, register as an external-trade operator with the Macao Customs Service under the Macao External Trade Act (Regime Jurídico do Comércio Externo, Decree-Law 66/95/M); submit the exporter/importer registration through the Macao Single Window and obtain the customs operator code. Step 4: For any foreign non-resident partner without a Macao address, appoint a Macao tax representative for service of process and tax notices; provide a certified translation into Chinese or Portuguese if required. Step 5: Confirm the pass-through treatment in the DSF taxpayer registration file, and open a local settlement account with a Macao authorized bank for Stripe/Authorize.Net treasury settlements; no VAT ID is issued because Macao does not operate a VAT system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Macao tax law does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. The FIP's pass-through status is derived from its partnership form and may be administratively confirmed by the DSF; partners are separately assessed in their own jurisdictions. Where any partner's jurisdiction applies hybrid mismatch rules, the partnership allocation must be independently tested under that jurisdiction's domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT, GST, or sales and use tax is imposed in Macao SAR; Macao applies a selective consumption tax to specified goods (tobacco, alcohol, fuel, and motor vehicles), a tourist tax on hospitality services, and stamp duty on enumerated documents. Collectible trading cards and the described commercial printing activity are outside the selective consumption tax schedule.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Macao has no VAT/GST turnover registration threshold. A foreign-invested partnership carrying on commercial activity in Macao must obtain Taxpayer Number status for Macao Complementary Tax when business activity starts, independent of turnover; an importer/exporter operator registration under the Macao External Trade Act is also turnover-independent.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Macao because Macao does not levy VAT/GST, so there is no 0% export VAT rate and no input VAT refund to claim. Exports of collectible trading cards are outside Macao's indirect tax net by default. The FIP may still need to provide Macao customs export proof, commercial invoices, and, where requested, certificates of origin so that the buyer or importing distributor can obtain VAT/GST relief, deferred import tax, or input-credit treatment in the destination jurisdiction. If printed cards are exported from mainland China to Macao, the mainland export VAT zero-rating/refund rules are governed by the mainland tax authorities and are not part of the Macao FIP's tax registration.

  • Commercial invoice issued under the Macao Commercial Code
  • Packing list with card quantity, unit value, and HS classification
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing physical departure from Macao territory
  • Certificate of origin issued by the Macao Economic and Technological Development Bureau or an authorized Macao chamber when required by the destination customs regime
  • Bank SWIFT credit advice or foreign-exchange settlement receipt showing export proceeds received in a Macao authorized bank account

Not applicable. Macao does not require real-time fiscal clearance, XML/API e-invoicing, or anti-fraud digital stamping for ordinary commercial invoices. The FIP must issue commercial invoices in accordance with the Macao Commercial Code and retain them for DSF audit. Export and import declarations must be filed electronically through the Macao Single Window; this is a customs clearance mandate, not a tax invoice clearance mandate.

No VAT clawback is applicable because Macao has no export VAT or refund mechanism. However, false export declarations, misdescribed goods, forged certificates of origin, or sham transport documents are subject to penalties under the Macao External Trade Act and the Macao Customs Code, including fines, forfeiture of goods, suspension or cancellation of the exporter/importer registration, and criminal liability for smuggling or document forgery. Failure to evidence the 180-day repatriation of export proceeds through acceptable bank records can trigger Monetary Authority of Macao administrative sanctions, bank account restrictions, and enhanced AML scrutiny under Law 2/2006; in diversion cases, goods illegally returned to Macao may be assessed for selective consumption tax or customs duty with retroactive penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Macao operates as a Special Administrative Territory with no central bank in the strict sense. The Monetary Authority of Macao backs the Macao Pataca against the Hong Kong dollar in a linked exchange-rate band, with HKD as de facto parallel settlement currency. For a collectible trading card foreign-invested partnership, the capital account is effectively open: non-resident partners may inject equity capital in freely convertible currencies, and no exchange surrender obligation applies. Current account transfers, including royalty payments, card artwork licensing fees, and trade settlements, are remitted freely through licensed institutions. Profit remittance faces no statutory withholding at partnership level, but partners must comply with source-country tax filings and Macao complementary tax and stamp duty obligations. The practical hurdle is not regulatory exchange control but documentary KYC and anti-money-laundering verification, because the Monetary Authority expects banks to validate beneficial ownership and the underlying goods flow for capital-and-goods mismatch. Cross-border RMB payments into Macao are typically processed through Bank of China (Macau) exercising the PRC clearing arrangement; however, US dollar and HKD wires via ordinary correspondent banks remain unrestricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender requirement exists for Macao-incorporated entities. All export receipts from collectible trading card sales may be retained in MOP, HKD, USD, or another freely chosen currency without mandatory conversion. Generating companies should still report export declarations through Macao Economic and Technological Development Bureau for trade statistics and maintain underlying contracts for banking due diligence.

Central bank supervision: Monetary Authority of Macao (Autoridade Monetária de Macau), with prudential coordination with the People's Bank of China for cross-border renminbi clearing

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Not viable as a bank replacement. Macao's financial system prohibits unlicensed payment institutions and e-money issuers from settling commercial collections for resident entities; Revolut, Stripe Treasury, or similar offshore EMIs cannot passport into Macao under current rules. Stripe and Authorize.Net may be used only as downstream card-processing interfaces that route into an acquiring account held by a Monetary Authority-licensed bank or licensed PSP in Macao, with settlement into the FIP's operational bank account. The FIP must therefore maintain a minimum of one Tier-1 Macao account to serve as the liquidity apex.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: MOP 3,000,000 in aggregate related-party transactions triggers the mandatory preparation of a Local File; a group-wide revenue above MOP 10,000,000 triggers a Master File. Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be maintained in Portuguese or English and submitted to the Macao Financial Services Bureau within 30 days of written request.

Economic substance scope: Macao's offshore tax regime under Decree-Law 58/99/M and the general anti-abuse principles require the FIP to maintain real economic substance: physical office premises in Macao, qualified employees, independent management, and substantive Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) performed in Macao. A letterbox partnership, passive payment-rail invoicing, or all key decisions exercised abroad will be re-characterized as a sham and will lose any treaty/incentive benefit, with the tax authority reconstructing taxable profits at full arm's-length margins.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Re-characterization under Article 12 of Decree-Law 58/99/M and the Macao Tax Code; immediate revocation of offshore/exempt status; assessment of 12% Complementary Tax on the full economic margin; surcharge interest of 2% per month; administrative fine up to MOP 1,000,000. The arrangement is treated as a sham tax-avoidance construction.

Director personal liability: Directors and managers who knowingly use the FIP as a shell are jointly and severally liable under Macao civil-law veil-piercing principles for the unpaid tax, penalties, interest and civil asset restitution. The court may impose a travel ban and blacklist the individual as a delinquent tax debtor; criminal liability for tax fraud/fraud can result in imprisonment of up to 3 years, disqualification as a director and freezing of personal assets.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Repatriation Evasion

Statutory sanction: Arm's-length adjustment under the Macao transfer pricing rules, with upward reassessment of taxable income; deficiency tax plus additional fine of up to 100% of the undercharged tax; administrative penalty between MOP 500,000 and MOP 2,000,000 for missing documentation; criminal tax evasion if false forms are filed.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false Form M/3 declarations are personally liable to restitution of the underpaid tax and penalty; they may be subject to asset attachment, travel ban and blacklisting. A director who directs or approves the sham royalty or margin scheme faces criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years for aggravated tax fraud, and is barred from corporate management in Macao for up to 10 years.
AML / Cross-Border Payment Rail Misuse

Statutory sanction: Violation of Macao AML Law No. 2/2006; money laundering predicate is punishable by 2 to 8 years' imprisonment; administrative AML fines of up to MOP 1,000,000 and revocation of financial payment-rail agreements; all tainted funds are forfeited; aggravated cases involving organized crime trigger enhanced penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors are treated as compliance officers of the regulated FIP and may have the corporate veil pierced to recover laundered amounts and lost tax. Civil asset restitution is ordered against personal estates; travel bans and blacklisting under the Macao AML Committee apply; imprisonment of up to 8 years can be imposed for directors who knowingly permit the payment-rail structure to conceal illicit proceeds or evade tax.
