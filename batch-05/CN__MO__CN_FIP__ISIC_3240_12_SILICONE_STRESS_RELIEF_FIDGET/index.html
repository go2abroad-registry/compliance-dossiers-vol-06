None — no statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for the Foreign-Invested Partnership; the founding partners fix the capital freely in the deed. No blocked escrow account, capital deposit certificate, or advance capital verification is required by Macao law before registration.

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None for PRC partnership tax: the Foreign-Invested Partnership is mandatorily fiscally transparent for Enterprise Income Tax, and partners are taxed on their distributive shares. There is no check-the-box election. For VAT, the FIP may elect small-scale taxpayer treatment while below the general taxpayer threshold, but general taxpayer registration is mandatory for export VAT zero-rating. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales in China (mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration); below this, voluntary registration is possible; a small-scale taxpayer exemption applies at monthly sales up to RMB 100,000 or quarterly sales up to RMB 300,000. No equivalent VAT registration threshold exists in Macao SAR.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim 0% VAT on export sales from mainland China to destinations outside the PRC customs territory (including Macao SAR), the CN_FIP must be a VAT general taxpayer, hold export business qualification in its registered scope, physically ship goods under an approved customs export declaration, receive sales proceeds in foreign currency or offshore RMB within the prescribed collection period, and retain all supporting trade and logistics documentation. Qualifying offshore digital services are zero-rated only where supplied to a non-resident recipient, consumed outside China and supported by service export contracts and payment records.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (electronic export customs declaration from the General Administration of Customs)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or multimodal transport document showing physical export movement
  • Export commercial invoice, packing list, and sales contract
  • Special VAT invoice for export goods, backed by input VAT invoices and purchase records
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt confirmation or SAFE export foreign-exchange collection record (equivalent to an inward remittance certificate)
  • Export Tax Rebate (Exemption) declaration submitted through the China Electronic Tax Bureau

China mandates electronic VAT invoicing through the STA's Golden Tax System and fully digitalized electronic invoice platform. All VAT invoices are issued using tax control software/UKey and transmitted in real time to the national electronic invoice service platform; bulk issuers must use STA-approved APIs. For export VAT rebate filings, invoice, customs export and SAFE foreign-exchange data must be matched through authorized digital interfaces. Macao SAR does not require real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping; commercial invoices satisfy DSF documentation.

Export zero-rating is cross-checked against customs export data, foreign-exchange collection records and VAT invoice flows. If documents are false, incomplete, or foreign exchange is not received within the required timeframe (subject to approved extended-collection filing), the refund is denied or clawed back, output VAT is re-imposed, late payment surcharge accrues at 0.05% per day, and administrative fines may reach 50% to 500% of the tax understated. Fraudulent export VAT refund claims trigger criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law, customs administrative penalties, public tax dishonesty listing and the dispersal of any Macao parallel tax benefits.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Macao SAR operates a free, open, and convertible financial system. The Pataca (MOP) is pegged to the Hong Kong dollar (HKD) at approximately MOP 1.03 per HKD, and the HKD itself is banded against the US dollar, making MOP/HKD/USD conversion highly stable and liquid. For a Chinese-linked FIP, the current account is fully convertible: export receipts from fidget-toy sales, consultancy fees, and e-commerce payouts may be settled in MOP, HKD, USD, or CNH at AMCM-licensed banks without prior approval. The capital account is also broadly open for inward direct investment into a foreign-invested partnership; no prior AMCM consent is needed for an equity contribution unless a regulated activity is involved, and no mandatory local reinvestment ratio is imposed. Profit remittance is equally permitted: declared profits may be distributed and wired out freely once audited accounts, partner resolutions, and Macao tax clearance are produced. The practical hurdles are therefore documentary, not quantitative: the bank must record the economic purpose of each inward payment, complete OFAC, EU, United Nations, and Macao AML screening, and any flow into mainland China must satisfy PRC SAFE and State Taxation Administration filing requirements on the mainland side. Because Macao is a separate customs and fiscal jurisdiction from mainland China, the treasury must keep a complete local accounting trail. Overall convertibility is high; the binding constraints are KYC documentation, sanctions screening, and cross-border RMB disclosures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender ratio or statutory conversion timeline applies in Macao. The FIP may retain 100% of its export foreign-currency proceeds in a licensed bank foreign-currency account, in any convertible currency, and may remit them abroad without forced internalization. The only obligation is the bank's reporting duty for transactions that exceed the large-transaction threshold established by AMCM under Macao's anti-money-laundering law, supported by the underlying export contract, customs declaration, and shipping documents.

Central bank supervision: Monetary Authority of Macao (AMCM), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China as the relevant mainland authority for cross-border RMB and capital-flow filings.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability rating: LOW. Macao's Financial System Act requires any entity undertaking deposit-taking, payment-account services, money transmission, or foreign-exchange conversion for customers to obtain a financial-institution licence from the AMCM. A non-resident fintech EMI chartered in Hong Kong, Singapore, the United Kingdom, or the European Union has no passporting or mutual-recognition right into Macao and cannot lawfully provide corporate accounts or clearing services to a Macao-incorporated FIP. Using an unlicensed offshore EMI would generate an automatic AML red flag, expose the general partner to criminal liability under Law 2/2006, and impair the auditor's capital verification. The FIP should therefore use only AMCM-licensed commercial banks and, only where necessary, licensed Macao payment-service operators for e-commerce payouts.

All inbound capital for the FIP must be routed through its corporate account held at an AMCM-licensed bank. Use SWIFT MT103 for customer-initiated credit transfers or MT202 for bank-to-bank cover payments, with the beneficiary account in the FIP's name and the field-70 remittance reference containing the contribution-agreement number and 'FIP-EQUITY' purpose code. If the remitter is in mainland China, the payer's bank must have a SAFE outward-remittance approval/form filing, and the payer must provide a State Taxation Administration tax clearance certificate to avoid a blocked remittance. After settlement, the account bank issues a stamped 'Inward Remittance Certificate' or 'Capital Contribution Confirmation'; a Macao-licensed CPA uses that certificate, together with the notarized contribution resolution, to produce the formal capital verification report. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability rating: LOW. Macao's Financial System Act requires any entity undertaking deposit-taking, payment-account services, money transmission, or foreign-exchange conversion for customers to obtain a financial-institution licence from the AMCM. A non-resident fintech EMI chartered in Hong Kong, Singapore, the United Kingdom, or the European Union has no passporting or mutual-recognition right into Macao and cannot lawfully provide corporate accounts or clearing services to a Macao-incorporated FIP. Using an unlicensed offshore EMI would generate an automatic AML red flag, expose the general partner to criminal liability under Law 2/2006, and impair the auditor's capital verification. The FIP should therefore use only AMCM-licensed commercial banks and, only where necessary, licensed Macao payment-service operators for e-commerce payouts.
